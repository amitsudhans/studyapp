Multiples

Counting Steps, Multiples & Common Multiples

Mentors: Eratosthenes of Cyrene & Euclid of Alexandria
Multiples arise when we multiply a number by whole counting numbers 1, 2, 3, ... In the classic classroom game, counting numbers divisible by 3 call 'Idli', numbers divisible by 5 call 'Vada', and numbers divisible by both (common multiples like 15, 30, 45) call 'Idli-Vada'!

What is a Multiple?

A multiple of a number is formed by multiplying that number by natural counting numbers: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ... For instance:

Multiples of 3 ('Idli'): 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30, ...
Multiples of 5 ('Vada'): 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, ...

Common Multiples: The 'Idli-Vada' Moments

When a number appears in both lists simultaneously, it is a common multiple. For 3 and 5, the first common multiple is 15. The subsequent ones are exact multiples of 15: 15, 30, 45, 60, 75, ...

Lowest Common Multiple (LCM): The smallest non-zero common multiple between two numbers is called their LCM. For 3 and 5, LCM(3, 5) = 15.

5.1 Factors Slide 3 of 20

Factors

Exact Divisors & Common Jump Sizes

Mentors: Eratosthenes of Cyrene & Euclid of Alexandria
A factor is an exact divisor that divides a number completely with zero remainder. In the Jump Jackpot game, jumping in step sizes of k lands on target number N if and only if k is a factor of N! Common factors are step sizes that land on two targets simultaneously.

What is a Factor?

A number k is a factor of N if N ÷ k leaves no remainder. In other words, you can multiply k by another whole number to obtain N.

Factors of 12 (Target A): 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 (Total 6 factors)
Factors of 18 (Target B): 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18 (Total 6 factors)

Common Factors & Highest Common Factor (HCF)

The jump sizes that land successfully on both 12 and 18 are: 1, 2, 3, 6. The greatest of these common jump sizes is 6, known as the Highest Common Factor (HCF) or Greatest Common Divisor (GCD): HCF(12, 18) = 6.

5.1 Special Numbers Slide 4 of 20

Perfect Numbers

When Proper Divisors Sum to the Whole

Mentors: Eratosthenes of Cyrene & Euclid of Alexandria
A perfect number is a positive integer whose proper positive factors (all factors excluding the number itself) add up exactly to the number. Equivalently, the sum of all its factors equals twice the number!

The Magic of 6 and 28

In ancient Greek mathematics, numbers whose parts add up harmoniously to themselves were celebrated as Perfect Numbers:

The First Perfect Number: 6 Factors of 6: 1, 2, 3, 6
Sum of proper factors: 1 + 2 + 3 = 6!
Sum of all factors: 1 + 2 + 3 + 6 = 12 = 2 × 6.
The Second Perfect Number: 28 Factors of 28: 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, 28
Sum of proper factors: 1 + 2 + 4 + 7 + 14 = 28!
Sum of all factors: 28 + 28 = 56 = 2 × 28.

The next perfect number is 496, followed by 8128! All known perfect numbers are even.

5.2 Sifting Primes Slide 6 of 20

Prime Sieve

The Ancient Grid Filter for Primes up to 100

Mentors: Eratosthenes of Cyrene & Euclid of Alexandria
Around 240 BCE, Greek mathematician Eratosthenes created a systematic algorithm to filter out all composite numbers up to 100. By crossing out 1, then circling 2 and crossing its multiples, circling 3 and crossing its multiples, and repeating for 5 and 7, exactly 25 primes emerge untouched!

How the Ancient Sieve Works

Write all numbers from 1 to 100 in a 10 × 10 grid:

  1. Cross out 1 (neither prime nor composite).
  2. Circle 2 (the only even prime!), cross out all other multiples of 2 (4, 6, 8, ...).
  3. Circle 3, cross out all remaining multiples of 3 (9, 15, 21, ...).
  4. Circle 5, cross out all remaining multiples of 5 (25, 35, 55, ...).
  5. Circle 7, cross out all remaining multiples of 7 (49, 77, 91).
Stop Criterion: √100 = 10! Since every composite up to 100 must have at least one prime factor ≤ 10 (namely 2, 3, 5, or 7), all uncrossed numbers remaining on the grid are guaranteed to be Primes! There are exactly 25 primes between 1 and 100.

Twin Primes

Prime Neighbors with a Difference of 2

Mentors: Eratosthenes of Cyrene & Euclid of Alexandria
Twin primes are pairs of prime numbers that differ by exactly 2. Famous pairs like (3, 5), (11, 13), and (17, 19) sit on either side of an even number. Patterns like Sophie Germain primes (doubling a prime and adding 1) open windows into modern cryptography.

What are Twin Primes?

Two prime numbers are called Twin Primes if their difference is exactly 2: p₂ − p₁ = 2.

5.3 Co-primes Slide 8 of 20

Co-primes

Safekeeping Treasures & Mutual Independence

Mentors: Eratosthenes of Cyrene & Euclid of Alexandria
Two numbers are co-prime (or relatively prime) if their ONLY common factor is 1. Neither number has to be prime on its own! For instance, 4 and 9 are both composite, yet they share no common factor other than 1, making them co-prime partners.

Definition of Co-prime Numbers

Two whole numbers a and b are called co-prime if: HCF(a, b) = 1 They share no common divisors greater than 1.

Surprising Fact: Both Can Be Composite!

Consider 4 and 9:

  • Factors of 4: {1, 2, 4} (Composite)
  • Factors of 9: {1, 3, 9} (Composite)
  • Common Factors: {1} only!
Therefore, 4 and 9 are CO-PRIME!
Consecutive Numbers Rule: Any two consecutive counting numbers (like 14 and 15, or 99 and 100) are ALWAYS co-prime!

5.3 Multiples of Co-primes Slide 10 of 20

Co-prime LCM

The Direct Product Property of Co-prime Numbers

Mentors: Eratosthenes of Cyrene & Euclid of Alexandria
When two numbers are co-prime, they share no internal prime factors. As a consequence, their very first common multiple (LCM) is equal to their direct product: LCM(a, b) = a × b. This rule provides a vital shortcut in algebra and fractions.

Direct Product Theorem

For any two co-prime numbers a and b: LCM(a, b) = a × b

Example 1: 5 and 7

5 and 7 are co-prime.

LCM(5, 7) = 5 × 7 = 35.

No smaller positive number is divisible by both.
Example 2: 4 and 9

4 and 9 are co-prime.

LCM(4, 9) = 4 × 9 = 36.

Smallest number in both multiple lists.
General Product Formula: For any two positive numbers: HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b. When HCF = 1, this simplifies directly to LCM = a × b!

Every composite number can be broken down step-by-step into smaller factors until every branch ends in an indivisible prime leaf. Whether you break 56 as 7 × 8 or 2 × 28, the final prime leaves are always identical: 2 × 2 × 2 × 7!

Constructing a Factor Tree

To find the prime factorisation of 56, pick any two factors to start:

Path 1: Starting with 7 × 8

56 = 7 × 8

Leaves: 2 × 2 × 2 × 7
Path 2: Starting with 2 × 28

56 = 2 × 28

28 = 2 × 14 = 2 × 2 × 7

Leaves: 2 × 2 × 2 × 7
Exponential Notation: We write repeated prime factors compactly using powers: 56 = 2³ × 7.

5.4 Prime DNA Slide 12 of 20

Prime DNA

Uniqueness of Prime Factorisation

Mentors: Eratosthenes of Cyrene & Euclid of Alexandria
The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic states that every whole number greater than 1 can be represented as a product of prime numbers in EXACTLY one way, apart from the order in which the factors are written. Primes are the unique atomic DNA of numbers!

Euclid's Master Theorem

In ancient Alexandria, Euclid proved that prime factorisation is unique:

Every integer n > 1 can be factored into primes uniquely, up to the rearrangement of terms.

12 = 2 × 2 × 3 = 2 × 3 × 2 = 3 × 2 × 2

Due to the commutative and associative laws of multiplication, the order does not matter: it always contains exactly two 2s and one 3!

Co-primes Through Prime Factorisation

Two numbers are co-prime if and only if they share zero common prime factors in their prime factorisations! For example, 15 = 3 × 5 and 28 = 2² × 7 share no common prime, so they are co-prime.

Factor Subsets

Testing Divisibility via Prime Subsets

Mentors: Eratosthenes of Cyrene & Euclid of Alexandria
A number A is divisible by another number B if and only if the complete prime factorisation of B is entirely contained within the prime factorisation of A. If B requires factors that A lacks, A cannot be divisible by B!

The Prime Ingredient Test

Think of prime factors as ingredients needed to bake a number. To divide A by B, the pantry of A must contain all the prime ingredients of B:

Case 1: Divisible!

Is 84 divisible by 14?

84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7

14 = 2 × 7

Both '2' and '7' are inside 84! ✓
Case 2: NOT Divisible!

Is 84 divisible by 8?

84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 (only two 2s)

8 = 2 × 2 × 2 (needs three 2s)

84 is missing one '2'! ✗

5.5 Divisibility Tests Slide 14 of 20

Rules: 2, 5, 10

Last Digit Shortcuts & Decimal Place Value

Mentors: Eratosthenes of Cyrene & Euclid of Alexandria
Because our number system is based on powers of 10, testing divisibility by factors of 10 (2, 5, and 10) requires checking ONLY the very last unit digit! This eliminates long division for even billion-digit numbers.

The Unit Digit Shortcuts

Every whole number can be split into its tens part plus its unit digit: Number = (Tens × 10) + Unit Digit. Since any multiple of 10 is already divisible by 2, 5, and 10, divisibility depends solely on the unit digit:

Divisible by 10: Unit digit must be strictly 0 (e.g., 40, 190, 4580).
Divisible by 5: Unit digit must be either 0 or 5 (e.g., 35, 80, 7925).
Divisible by 2: Unit digit must be an even digit: 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8 (e.g., 14, 58, 936).

5.5 Powers of 2 Slide 15 of 20

Rules: 4 & 8

Inspecting the Last 2 and Last 3 Digits

Mentors: Eratosthenes of Cyrene & Euclid of Alexandria
Since 100 is divisible by 4, any hundreds, thousands, and higher digits contribute nothing to the remainder when dividing by 4! Hence, divisibility by 4 depends ONLY on the last 2 digits. Similarly, since 1000 is divisible by 8, divisibility by 8 depends ONLY on the last 3 digits.

Divisibility by 4 (Last 2 Digits)

Because 100 = 4 × 25, any number of hundreds is automatically divisible by 4. Therefore, test only the number formed by its last two digits:

Is 7,536 divisible by 4?

Look at last two digits: 36.

36 ÷ 4 = 9 (Zero remainder!). So 7,536 is divisible by 4! ✓

Divisibility by 8 (Last 3 Digits)

Because 1000 = 8 × 125, any number of thousands is automatically divisible by 8. Test only the number formed by its last three digits:

Is 98,128 divisible by 8?

Look at last three digits: 128.

128 ÷ 8 = 16 (Zero remainder!). So 98,128 is divisible by 8! ✓
