6.1 Perimeter Foundations Slide 1 of 20

Boundary Walk

Total Distance Enclosing a Plane Figure

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
Perimeter is the total continuous distance covered when walking once entirely along the outer boundary of a closed plane figure. For any polygon, perimeter is simply the sum of the lengths of all its straight boundary sides.

What is Perimeter?

Imagine an ant walking along the outer edge of a closed geometric shape, starting at one corner and returning to where it began. The total distance walked is the Perimeter (from Greek peri = around, metron = measure).

General Polygon Rule:

Perimeter = Sum of all side lengths

For a 4-sided field: a + b + c + d
Real-World Examples:
  • Barbed wire fencing around a crop farm
  • Decorative lace border around a dupatta
  • Distance run around a sports track
Essential Condition: The figure must be closed! An open boundary has no defined perimeter.

6.1 Standard Formulas Slide 2 of 20

Perimeter Formulas

Shortcuts for Rectangles and Squares

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
Because opposite sides of a rectangle are equal in length, its perimeter equals 2 × (length + breadth). Because all four sides of a square are identical, its perimeter equals 4 × side length.

Deducing the Formulas

Instead of adding all four sides individually, symmetries provide elegant multiplication shortcuts:

Rectangle (Length l, Breadth b):

Opposite sides are equal: l + b + l + b = 2l + 2b

Perimeter = 2 × (length + breadth) = 2(l + b)
Square (Side s):

All four sides are equal: s + s + s + s = 4s

Perimeter = 4 × side = 4s
Finding the Side from Perimeter: If a square has perimeter P, each side length is: side = P ÷ 4.

6.1 Equal Sides & Angles Slide 3 of 20

Regular Polygons

Equilateral Triangles, Pentagons & Beyond

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
A polygon is regular if all its sides are equal in length and all its interior angles are equal. The perimeter of any regular polygon with n sides is simply n multiplied by the length of one side.

The General Regular Polygon Rule

When all sides of an n-sided polygon have equal length s: Perimeter = n × side length

Equilateral Triangle

n = 3

P = 3 × s
Regular Pentagon

n = 5

P = 5 × s
Regular Hexagon

n = 6

P = 6 × s
Crucial Distinction: A rhombus has 4 equal sides, but unequal angles, so it is equilateral but not regular. A square has both equal sides and equal angles, so it is a regular quadrilateral.

6.1 Real-World Application Slide 4 of 20

Track Stagger Puzzle

Concentric Lanes & Fair Starting Lines

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
In sports running tracks, outer lanes have larger perimeters than inner lanes. To ensure all runners run the exact same total distance to a common finish line, outer runners must be given staggered starting positions ahead of the inner runners!

The Geometry of Running Lanes

Consider two square running tracks where the outer track is 1 meter wider on all sides than the inner track:

Inner Track (Side = 20 m):

Perimeter = 4 × 20 = 80 m

Runner A runs 80 meters per lap.
Outer Track (Side = 22 m):

Perimeter = 4 × 22 = 88 m

Runner B runs 88 meters per lap!
The Fair Stagger: The outer runner's track is 88 − 80 = 8 meters longer. To make the race fair, Runner B must start 8 meters ahead of Runner A!

6.1 Measurement Units Slide 5 of 20

Dot Grid Perimeter

Straight Units (s) vs Diagonal Units (d)

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
When measuring paper cutouts on dot grids, boundaries consist of two distinct types of steps: straight unit steps between horizontally or vertically adjacent dots (s), and slanted diagonal steps across grid squares (d). Because d is longer than s, we record perimeters as combinations like 6s + 3d.

Straight vs Diagonal Grid Steps

On a standard square dot grid:

Straight Step (s):

Distance between adjacent dots horizontally or vertically = 1 unit (s).

Diagonal Step (d):

Distance corner-to-corner across a square = d ≈ 1.414 units.

Diagonal d is strictly longer than straight s!
Combined Perimeter Notation: If a shape has 6 straight edges and 4 diagonal edges on the dot grid, its exact boundary length is written as: Perimeter = 6s + 4d.

6.1 Conservation Principle Slide 6 of 20

Split & Rejoin

Perimeter Changes While Area Stays Constant

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
Cutting a geometric shape into pieces and rearranging them into a new configuration preserves the total material and surface area, but can drastically alter the total perimeter! Internal cuts expose new outer edges that increase boundary length.

The Split-and-Rejoin Experiment

Take a square paper of side 4 cm:

Original Square (4 × 4):

Area = 4 × 4 = 16 cm²

Perimeter = 4 × 4 = 16 cm

Cut in Half & Joined into (8 × 2):

Area = 8 × 2 = 16 cm² (Same!)

Perimeter = 2(8 + 2) = 20 cm (Increased!)

Core Insight: Area is conserved under cutting and rearranging, but perimeter is NOT conserved! Whenever an internal cut is pulled apart, two new boundary edges are created.

6.2 Area Foundations Slide 7 of 20

Area & Grid Units

Why We Measure Surface in Unit Squares

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
Area is the measure of the enclosed flat region bounded by a shape, expressed in square units (cm², m²). We use square units because squares tile flat surfaces seamlessly without leaving any empty gaps, unlike circular tiles which leave unfilled holes.

What is Area?

While perimeter measures the boundary edge line, Area measures the enclosed flat surface inside the boundary.

Unit Square (1 sq unit):

A square with side length of 1 unit. Area of a 1 cm square is written as 1 sq cm (or 1 cm²).

Why NOT Circles?

If you try to cover a floor with circular coins, they leave diamond-shaped gaps between them! Squares tile without gaps.

Tiling Property: Shapes that fill a plane completely without overlapping or leaving gaps are called tessellations. Squares, equilateral triangles, and regular hexagons can tile the plane; circles cannot.

Area Formulas

Multiplication Rules & Multi-Step Practical Problems

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
Area of a rectangle is calculated by length × breadth, and area of a square is side × side. Practical real-life problems involve multi-step arithmetic, such as finding uncarpeted border floors or remaining field areas after digging corner flower beds.

Standard Formulas

In a grid of l columns and b rows:

Rectangle Area: Area = length × breadth = l × b
Square Area: Area = side × side = s²

Multi-Step Subtraction Problem: Uncarpeted Floor

Room is 5 m long × 4 m wide. Total Floor Area = 5 × 4 = 20 m².

A square carpet of side 3 m is laid on the floor. Carpet Area = 3 × 3 = 9 m².

Uncarpeted Area = Total Floor − Carpet = 20 − 9 = 11 m².

Tangram Exploration

7-Piece Puzzle & Relative Area Ratios

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
The ancient Chinese 7-piece Tangram consists of 2 large triangles, 1 medium triangle, 2 small triangles, 1 square, and 1 parallelogram. Comparing relative piece sizes reveals that the 2 small triangles together equal the area of the square, the parallelogram, and the medium triangle!

The 7 Tangram Pieces & Their Relative Units

Let the area of one small triangle = 1 unit. Then:

Small Triangle (×2) 1 unit each
Tangram Square 2 units
Parallelogram 2 units
Medium Triangle 2 units

Large Triangles (×2): Each large triangle equals 4 units.

Total Tangram Square Area = 1 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 4 + 4 = 16 units!

6.2 Irregular Shapes Slide 10 of 20

Estimating on Grids

The 4-Step Standard Graph Paper Counting Rule

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
To estimate the area of irregular natural shapes (such as a fallen leaf or hand outline) placed over 1 cm² graph paper, follow the universal 4-step counting protocol: full squares count as 1, portions greater than half count as 1, exact halves count as 0.5, and portions less than half are ignored.

The Universal Graph Paper Protocol

When an irregular boundary cuts across grid squares:

1. Fully filled grid square: Count as 1 sq unit
2. More than half filled: Count as 1 sq unit
3. Exactly half filled: Count as 0.5 sq unit
4. Less than half filled: Ignore (Count as 0)

Why ignore less than half while counting more than half as 1? Because the small ignored portions statistically balance out the rounded-up portions!

6.2 Area vs Perimeter Dynamics Slide 11 of 20

Same Area, Varied Perimeters

How Shape Proportions Govern Boundary Length

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
Rectangles with the exact same fixed area can have drastically different perimeters! Long, skinny rectangular strips have massive perimeters, while compact, squarish shapes minimize perimeter for the same enclosed area.

Fixed Area = 24 sq units

Examine all whole-number factor pairs that form an area of 24 cm²:

1 × 24 Rectangle P = 2(1 + 24) P = 50 cm
2 × 12 Rectangle P = 2(2 + 12) P = 28 cm
3 × 8 Rectangle P = 2(3 + 8) P = 22 cm
4 × 6 Rectangle P = 2(4 + 6) P = 20 cm
The Compactness Principle: As the rectangle becomes closer to a square (sides more equal: 4 and 6), its perimeter drops from 50 cm to 20 cm! Squarish shapes enclose the most area with the least fencing.

6.2 Perimeter Surgery Slide 12 of 20

Making It More or Less

Adding Unit Squares to Alter Perimeter

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
Attaching a new unit square to a figure always increases the total area by 1 sq unit. However, whether the perimeter increases, decreases, or stays the same depends entirely on where you attach it: attaching at an edge adds +2 edges, inside an indentation can decrease perimeter!

Attaching a 1 × 1 Square to a Boundary

Every 1 × 1 unit square has 4 edges. When you stick it onto an existing shape:

1. Attached to a Flat Edge (1 shared edge):

Covers 1 existing edge, but exposes 3 new outer edges. Net change: 3 − 1 = +2 units!

2. Tucked into a Corner Indentation (2 shared edges):

Covers 2 existing edges and exposes 2 new outer edges. Net change: 2 − 2 = 0 (Perimeter Unchanged!)

3. Slotting into a 3-Sided Pocket (3 shared edges):

Covers 3 existing edges and exposes only 1 new outer edge. Net change: 1 − 3 = −2 units (Perimeter Decreases!)

Charan & Sharan's House Room Areas

Architects represent buildings through 2D floor plans. Finding missing wall dimensions, individual room areas (living room, kitchen, bedroom), and total house footprints requires decomposing composite floor plans into separate rectangles.

Decomposing a House Floor Plan

A typical house plan consists of adjoining rectangular rooms:

Living Room (6 m × 4 m): Area = 24 m²
Master Bedroom (4 m × 3 m): Area = 12 m²
Kitchen (3 m × 3 m): Area = 9 m²

6.2 Geometric Deduction Slide 14 of 20

Area Maze Puzzles

Japanese Menseki Meiro Deduction

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
Area Mazes (Menseki Meiro) are Japanese geometric logic puzzles. By using the relationship Area = length × breadth, you deduce missing side lengths and unknown regional areas step-by-step without using complicated fractions or decimals!

How Area Mazes Work

Every section is a rectangle with whole-number dimensions:

Clue 1: Rectangle A has Area = 18 cm², Width = 3 cm

Length = 18 ÷ 3 = 6 cm

Clue 2: Adjacent Rectangle B shares this 6 cm side and has Area = 24 cm²

Other side = 24 ÷ 6 = 4 cm

The Secret: Never guess or measure with a ruler! Use division (Length = Area ÷ Width) to unlock adjacent cells step-by-step.

6.3 Triangle Formula Slide 15 of 20

Area of a Triangle

Diagonal Folding & Half-Rectangle Derivation

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
Drawing a diagonal across any rectangle divides it into two congruent, identical right-angled triangles of equal area. Therefore, the area of each triangle is exactly half the area of the enclosing rectangle: Area = 1/2 × base × height!

Deriving the Triangle Formula

Take a rectangle with base b and height h:

Area of enclosing rectangle = b × h
The diagonal splits it into 2 equal congruent triangles.
The Universal Triangle Formula: Area = (1/2) × base × height = (b × h) ÷ 2
Works for Any Triangle: Even for non-right triangles, dropping a perpendicular height splits the triangle into two right triangles, confirming that Area = 1/2 × b × h always holds!
Checkpoint A right triangle has base 8 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm. What is its area?
Checkpoint A rectangle of dimensions 10 cm × 6 cm is cut along its diagonal into two triangles. What is the area of each triangle?
Triangle Area Derivation Area = 1/2 × b × h
Triangle = (10 × 6) ÷ 2 = 30 cm²
6.3 Spatial Decomposition Slide 16 of 20

Composite Grid Shapes

Decomposition into Rectangles and Triangles

Mentors: Acharya Baudhāyana & Archimedes of Syracuse
Complex polygons drawn on square grids can be measured with exact mathematical precision by decomposing them into a patchwork of simple non-overlapping rectangles and right triangles, then summing their individual areas.

The Divide-and-Conquer Technique

To find the area of an arrow, house outline, or irregular polygon on a grid:

  1. Draw horizontal or vertical slice lines to partition the shape into familiar pieces.
  2. Calculate each rectangle piece via length × breadth.
  3. Calculate each triangular corner via (1/2) × base × height.
  4. Add all piece areas together for the exact total!
Example: House Shape (Wall + Roof)

Wall Rectangle (4 × 3) = 12 sq units

Roof Triangle (base 4, height 2) = (1/2) × 4 × 2 = 4 sq units

Total House Area = 12 + 4 = 16 sq units!
