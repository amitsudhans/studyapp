Common Difference d

Defining Arithmetic Progressions, General Form a, a+d, a+2d... & Finite vs Infinite APs

Slide 2 of 20
👨‍🏫
Classroom Teacher Spoken Lecture Pure Indian Male Teacher Sound

Now lock down the crisp formal definition from Section Five Point Two! An Arithmetic Progression, abbreviated as AP, is a list of numbers in which each term is obtained by adding a fixed number d to its immediately preceding term, except for the very first term a! That fixed step d wears the title common difference. Look closely: can the common difference d only be a positive number? Never fall for that common myth! Common difference d can comfortably be positive, like 1, 2, 3, 4 where d equals plus 1; negative, like 100, 70, 40, 10 where d equals minus 30; or even zero, like 3, 3, 3, 3 where d equals zero! Writing the general form algebraically gives first term a, second term a plus d, third term a plus 2 d, fourth term a plus 3 d, and onward! Furthermore, if an AP stops cleanly at a clear last term, like 11 wooden ladder rungs, it is called a finite AP. However, if dots trail endlessly onward forever without a final term, it is called an infinite AP! Always verify any candidate sequence carefully by checking whether each term minus its preceding term stays strictly identical across every single consecutive pair!

General nth Term

Deriving Formula aₙ = a + (n − 1)d and Finding Specific Terms (30th, 11th, etc.)

Suppose a sequence starts at 2 with constant step 5, giving 2, 7, 12, 17, 22, and someone demands the 30th or 100th term instantly without writing 99 additions by hand! Look closely at the counting pattern built by adding d repeatedly starting from first term a. Term 1 equals a plus zero steps of d. Term 2 equals a plus one step of d. Term 3 equals a plus two steps of d. Notice the exact offset every single time: to reach Term Number n, how many forward jumps of common difference d do you take starting from Term 1? Always one less than n, meaning precisely n minus 1 steps! That insight gives our golden nth Term Formula: a sub n equals a plus n minus 1 times d! Test it immediately on Exercise Five Point Two to find the 30th term of the AP 10, 7, 4, and so on! Identify first term a equals 10 and common difference d equals 7 minus 10 equals minus 3. Plug n equals 30 cleanly into the formula: Term 30 equals 10 plus 30 minus 1 times minus 3, which is 10 plus 29 times minus 3, giving 10 minus 87 equals minus 77 with instant precision!

Now turn Gauss's brilliant pairing trick into two sparkling algebraic formulas working for any Arithmetic Progression! Write sum S sub n forward from a and a plus d up to final term a plus n minus 1 times d, then write S sub n completely reversed underneath! Add vertically: every single one of the n columns sums identically to 2 a plus n minus 1 times d! Dividing twice S sub n by 2 delivers Formula One: S sub n equals n over 2 times 2 a plus n minus 1 times d! Look how neatly 2 a plus n minus 1 times d splits inside into a plus a plus n minus 1 times d, which equals first term a plus last term l! That yields Formula Two: whenever first term a and last term l are known, S sub n equals simply n over 2 times a plus l!
