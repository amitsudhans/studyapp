Section 6.1 & 6.2 • Foundation

Congruence vs. Similarity: Shapes in Proportion

Two figures are congruent if they have the same shape AND the same size. Two figures are similar (~) if they have the same shape, but not necessarily the same size!

🔭

"All congruent figures are similar, but similar figures need NOT be congruent!"
⭕ 🔴

All Circles are Similar

Every circle has the exact same circular shape, differing only by radius.

🔲 ⬛

All Squares are Similar

All 4 angles are 90° and all 4 sides are in identical 1:1 proportion.

🔺 🔺

All Equilateral Triangles

All angles are strictly 60° and all 3 sides scale equally.

All angles are equal (90°), but sides are NOT proportional ⟹ NOT similar!

Section 6.3 • Theorem 6.1 (Thales Theorem)

The Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT)

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, the other two sides are divided in the same ratio: AD / DB = AE / EC.

🏛️
Thales of Miletus
640 – 546 B.C. • Greek Geometer
DE ∥ BC ⟹ AD / DB = AE / EC
Theorem 6.1 Statement:
AD / DB = AE / EC

Example: If AD = 1.5 cm, DB = 3 cm, and AE = 1 cm ⟹ EC = 2 cm!

Important Corollaries (Adding 1 to both sides):
1. AD / AB = AE / AC (Upper part over whole side)
2. DB / AB = EC / AC (Lower part over whole side)
NCERT Page 80 • High-Scoring Board Proof

Step-by-Step Proof of Theorem 6.1

Proven using the ratio of triangle areas: Area = (1/2) × base × height.

Step 1: Altitudes & Left Side

Draw EN ⊥ AB and DM ⊥ AC. Join BE & CD.

ar(ADE) = 1/2 × AD × EN
ar(BDE) = 1/2 × DB × EN
⟹ ar(ADE) / ar(BDE) = AD / DB --- (1)
Step 2: Right Side Ratio

Using altitude DM on AC:

ar(ADE) = 1/2 × AE × DM
ar(DEC) = 1/2 × EC × DM
⟹ ar(ADE) / ar(DEC) = AE / EC --- (2)
Step 3: Equal Area & Conclusion

ΔBDE and ΔDEC share same base DE between parallels DE ∥ BC:

ar(BDE) = ar(DEC) --- (3)
Therefore: AD / DB = AE / EC !
Q.E.D. Hence Proved!
NCERT Section 6.3 • Theorem 6.2

Converse of BPT: Proving Lines are Parallel

If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side.

Proof by Contradiction

Given: AD / DB = AE / EC
Assume DE is NOT parallel to BC. Draw DE' ∥ BC.
By BPT: AD / DB = AE' / E'C
⟹ AE / EC = AE' / E'C
Adding 1 to both sides: (AE + EC)/EC = (AE' + E'C)/E'C
⟹ AC / EC = AC / E'C ⟹ EC = E'C!
Therefore, E and E' must coincide ⟹ DE ∥ BC!

Application: Trapezium ABCD (Fig 6.14)

ABCD is a trapezium with AB ∥ DC and EF ∥ AB.

Join AC to meet EF at point G.
In ΔADC: EG ∥ DC ⟹ AE / ED = AG / GC (by BPT)
In ΔCAB: GF ∥ AB ⟹ BF / FC = AG / GC (by BPT)
⟹ AE / ED = BF / FC!
NCERT Section 6.4 • Theorem 6.3

AAA & AA Similarity Criterion

If in two triangles, corresponding angles are equal, then their corresponding sides are in the same ratio, and the triangles are similar!

The AA Corollary

Why 2 Angles are Sufficient!

Since the sum of angles in any triangle is always 180°, if two angles of ΔABC equal two angles of ΔDEF, the third angle is automatically equal!

If ∠A = ∠D and ∠B = ∠E ⟹ ΔABC ~ ΔDEF (by AA)
Hourglass Example (Fig 6.29):

If PQ ∥ RS in bowtie/hourglass shape:

∠P = ∠S (Alternate interior angles)
∠Q = ∠R (Alternate interior angles)
∠POQ = ∠SOR (Vertically opposite angles)
⟹ ΔPOQ ~ ΔSOR (by AAA Similarity!)
NCERT Section 6.4 • Theorem 6.4

SSS Similarity: Proportional Sides Mean Equal Angles

If corresponding sides of two triangles are proportional, then their corresponding angles are equal, and the triangles are similar.

🔍 NCERT Example 5: Find ∠P from Triangles ABC and PQR (Fig 6.30)

Given sides: AB = 3.8, BC = 6, CA = 3√3; RQ = 7.6, QP = 12, PR = 6√3; and ∠A = 80°, ∠B = 60°.

Compare Ratios:
AB / RQ = 3.8 / 7.6 = 1/2
BC / QP = 6 / 12 = 1/2
CA / PR = 3√3 / 6√3 = 1/2
Symbolic Similarity:
All ratios equal 1/2 ⟹
ΔABC ~ ΔRQP (by SSS)
⟹ ∠C = ∠P
Calculate ∠P:
∠C = 180° - 80° - 60° = 40°
⟹ ∠P = 40°!
NCERT Section 6.4 • Theorem 6.5

SAS Similarity: Side-Angle-Side

If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of another triangle and the sides including these angles are proportional, then the two triangles are similar!

Theorem 6.5 Condition:
1. AB / DE = AC / DF
2. ∠A = ∠D (Included Angle)
⟹ ΔABC ~ ΔDEF (by SAS)

The angle MUST be strictly included between the two proportional sides.

NCERT Example 6:
Given: OA · OB = OC · OD
⟹ OA / OC = OD / OB
Also ∠AOD = ∠COB (Vertically opposite)
⟹ ΔAOD ~ ΔCOB (by SAS)
⟹ ∠A = ∠C and ∠B = ∠D!
NCERT Page 92-93 • Example 7 & Exercise 6.3 Q15

Real-World Shadow Modeling: Indirect Measurement

Because sun rays and lamp light travel in straight lines, heights and shadow lengths form similar triangles!

Example 7: Girl Walking Away from Lamp-Post

Lamp height = 3.6 m. Girl height = 90 cm = 0.9 m. Speed = 1.2 m/s for 4 s.

Walking distance BD = 1.2 × 4 = 4.8 m
Let shadow length DE = x metres
ΔABE ~ ΔCDE (by AA Similarity)
BE / DE = AB / CD ⟹ (4.8 + x) / x = 3.6 / 0.9 = 4
4.8 + x = 4x ⟹ 3x = 4.8
⟹ x = 1.6 m (Shadow length)

Exercise 6.3 Q15: Tower Height Problem

A 6 m pole casts a 4 m shadow. At the same time, a tower casts a 28 m shadow.

Sun angle of elevation is identical for both!
Height / Shadow = Constant
h / 28 = 6 / 4 = 1.5
h = 28 × 1.5 = 28 + 14
⟹ Height of Tower = 42 m!
