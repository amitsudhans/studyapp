Memorize the universal change formula carefully: Percentage Increase or Decrease equals Amount of Change divided by the ORIGINAL BASE, multiplied by 100! Slide 10 of 20

Follow any everyday notebook from factory floor to retail shelf along the commercial supply chain: Manufacturer to Wholesaler to Retailer to Customer! Three crucial price terms govern commerce in Section One Point Three: Cost Price (CP) is what the seller originally pays; Marked Price (MP) is the printed tag price before any discount; and Selling Price (SP) is the actual price paid by the buyer! Whenever SP exceeds CP, the seller earns a PROFIT (SP − CP)! Whenever SP drops below CP, the seller suffers a LOSS (CP − SP)! Suppose a buyer purchases an item at ₹1,000 Selling Price carrying 18% GST! In intra-state transactions, GST splits cleanly into 9% Central GST (CGST = ₹90) plus 9% State GST (SGST = ₹90), producing a final bill of ₹1,180!

Simple Compound Growth

Section 1.3 Linear Growth p(1 + rt) vs Compounding Growth p(1 + r)^t in Finance & Biology

Slide 11 of 20
👨‍🏫
Classroom Teacher Spoken Lecture Pure Indian Male Teacher Sound

Discover why growth repeated over multiple time periods behaves dramatically differently under COMPOUNDING versus NON-COMPOUNDING! Suppose we begin with initial principal amount p and steady percentage growth rate r per period (written as decimal r, such as 10% = 0.10) across t equal time periods! Without compounding (linear simple growth), ONLY the original principal p generates new growth during every single period! After t periods, cumulative gain equals p × r × t, producing Total Amount = p(1 + rt)! Invest ₹1,000 at 10% simple annual growth for 3 years: each passing year adds an identical +₹100, reaching exactly ₹1,300! Now activate COMPOUNDING! Under compounding exponential growth, each completed period's gain merges directly INTO the new base before calculating the NEXT period's increase—so previous growth itself earns further growth! Year One reaches p(1 + r)! Year Two multiplies THAT updated total by (1 + r) again, producing p(1 + r)²! After t periods, Total Amount = p(1 + r)^t! Test ₹1,000 at 10% compounded annually for 3 full years: ₹1,000 → ₹1,100 → ₹1,210 → ₹1,331! That extra +₹31 compounding snowball accelerates dramatically over many years in growing human populations and rapidly multiplying bacterial cultures!

Asset Value Decay

Section 1.3 Decline & Depreciation: Vehicles, Electronics & Exponential Decay p(1 - r)^t

Slide 12 of 20
👨‍🏫
Classroom Teacher Spoken Lecture Pure Indian Male Teacher Sound

Percentage compounding also operates cleanly in reverse! Everyday manufactured assets such as motor vehicles, smartphones, laptops, and even shrinking town populations steadily lose value or size over time—an important downward process called DEPRECIATION or DECLINE! Notice how identical multiplicative mathematics mirrors compounding growth, simply replacing +r with −r! Starting from initial asset value p and constant annual depreciation rate r (expressed as a decimal), each year preserves fraction (1 − r) of the previous year's remaining worth, giving Value V = p(1 − r)^t after t years! Test a brand-new laptop or scooter purchased for ₹50,000 depreciating at 20% annually! After Year One, it retains 80% (factor 0.80): 50,000 × 0.80 = ₹40,000 (dropping ₹10,000)! After Year Two, apply 20% decay directly TO ₹40,000, NEVER ₹50,000: 40,000 × 0.80 = ₹32,000 (dropping only ₹8,000)! After Year Three: 32,000 × 0.80 = ₹25,600! Spot this vital mathematical distinction clearly: whereas naive linear subtraction (20% × 3 = 60%) crashes straight down to just ₹20,000, realistic exponential annual depreciation p(1 − r)^t shrinks by progressively smaller rupee amounts during each consecutive year and smoothly approaches zero without ever touching zero for real assets across finite time periods!
