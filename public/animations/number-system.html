🌌 The Hierarchy of Real Numbers (R)

Explore how Natural, Whole, Integers, Rationals, and Irrationals nest together to form the Real Line.

Step 1: Natural Numbers (N) ℕ

Counting Numbers We Use in Daily Life

Natural numbers are the positive counting integers beginning from 1, 2, 3, 4, ... up to infinity. You use them when counting apples, books, or chairs.

Examples in this set:
1 2 3 100 10,000
💡 CBSE Key Takeaway: Every Natural Number is a Whole Number, an Integer, and a Rational Number!

📐 Constructing Irrational Numbers on the Number Line

Watch the animated compass and Pythagoras theorem locate √2 (≈1.414) and √5 on the number line.

Construct:
1 Drawing Base OA = 1 unit on the Number Line
OB² = 1² + 1² = 2 ⇒ OB = √2

Step 1: The Right Triangle

Take OA = 1 unit on the number line. At A, draw perpendicular AB = 1 unit. Join OB.

Step 2: Pythagoras Theorem

OB = √(OA^2 + AB^2) = √(1^2 + 1^2) = √(2). The hypotenuse length is exactly √(2).

Step 3: Compass Arc

With center O and radius OB, cut an arc on the number line at point P. Point P represents √(2) ≈ 1.414!

🔄 Decimal Expansions: Terminating, Repeating & Conversion to p/q

Understand the difference between rational and irrational decimals, and watch step-by-step algebra convert repeating decimals back to fractions.

Rational Numbers (Q) Can be p/q
  • Terminating: Remainder becomes 0 (e.g. 7/8 = 0.875, 1/2 = 0.5).
  • Non-terminating Recurring: Digits repeat forever (e.g. 1/3 = 0.333… = 0.3̄, 1/7 = 0.142857̄).
Irrational Numbers Cannot be p/q
  • Non-terminating & Non-recurring: Decimal never stops and never repeats in a pattern!
  • Examples: √(2) = 1.41421356…, π = 3.14159265…, 0.1010010001…

📝 Express Repeating Decimal 0.6̄ (0.666…) or 0.47̄ (0.4777…) as p/q

⚡ Rationalizing the Denominator

When an expression contains a square root in the denominator, multiply by its conjugate so the denominator becomes a rational integer.

The Golden Identity:
(a + √b)(a - √b) = a² - (√b)² = a² - b
The radical vanishes because the difference of squares eliminates the middle terms!
Problem 1 (Single Root) 1 / √2
1 / √2 × (√2 / √2)
= (1 × √2) / (√2 × √2)
= √2 / 2 ✓
Problem 2 (Conjugate) 1 / (√7 - √6)
Multiply numerator & denominator by conjugate (√7 + √6):
= (√7 + √6) / [(√7)² - (√6)²]
= (√7 + √6) / (7 - 6) = √7 + √6 ✓
