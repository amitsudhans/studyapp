Welcome to Chapter Ten: How Quantities Combine: Understanding Data! Every day, scientists, economists, teachers, and engineers combine separate datasets into one meaningful summary number! Recall how you already compute a simple arithmetic mean: add all individual values together and divide by the total count! Understanding weights prevents misleading statistics across science, finance, sports analytics, and public policy decisions every single day!

Combined Average Formula

Section 10.1.1 General Formulation: (an + bm)/(n + m) & When Simple Averaging Holds

Slide 3 of 20
👨‍🏫
Classroom Teacher Spoken Lecture Pure Indian Male Teacher Sound

Now formalize Section Ten Point One Point One into an exact algebraic theorem! Suppose Collection One contains n items with arithmetic average a, and Collection Two contains m items with arithmetic average b! What is the sum of all items inside Collection One? Since average equals sum divided by count, Sum One equals a TIMES n! Similarly, Sum Two equals b TIMES m! Now merge both collections into one big pool of n plus m items! The combined sum equals a n plus b m, so the true COMBINED AVERAGE is EXACTLY a n plus b m, ALL DIVIDED BY n plus m! Now ask an essential algebraic question: WHEN does this true combined average equal the naive simple average open a plus b close divided by two? Set the two fractions equal and cross-multiply! Two a n plus two b m equals a n plus a m plus b n plus b m! Rearranging terms cleanly gives open a minus b close TIMES open n minus m close EQUALS ZERO! Therefore, taking the simple average of two averages works ONLY in two situations: either both collections have the EXACT SAME SIZE (n equals m), OR both collections already have the EXACT SAME AVERAGE (a equals b)!

Weighted Mean Formula

Section 10.1.2 General Weighted Mean: x̄ = (W₁x₁ + W₂x₂ + ... + Wₙxₙ) / (W₁ + W₂ + ... + Wₙ)

Slide 6 of 20
👨‍🏫
Classroom Teacher Spoken Lecture Pure Indian Male Teacher Sound

Now unite all previous cases—group averages, liquid mixtures, and gold alloys—into ONE single master equation in Section Ten Point One Point Two: THE GENERAL WEIGHTED MEAN FORMULA! Whenever you have n values x one, x two, up to x sub n, carrying corresponding non-negative weights W one, W two, up to W sub n (with total weight positive), the weighted mean x-bar equals W one x one plus W two x two plus dot-dot-dot plus W sub n x sub n, ALL DIVIDED BY W one plus W two plus dot-dot-dot plus W sub n! Notice two super-handy properties of this formula! First, if you multiply EVERY weight by the same positive scaling factor—say converting all weights from grams to kilograms, or turning ratios three-to-two into percentages sixty percent and forty percent—the weighted mean stays COMPLETELY UNCHANGED because the common factor cancels cleanly from numerator and denominator! Second, the weighted mean ALWAYS lies strictly between the smallest data value and the largest data value! If every value in a mixture lies between ten and thirty, no combination of positive weights can ever push the weighted average below ten or above thirty—it always stays strictly bounded inside the data range!

Watch out for the single biggest reading mistake students make on stacked bar charts inside Section Ten Point Two Point One! Suppose a stacked column for Year Twenty Twenty-Five shows three stacked bands: Band A (bottom, blue) running from zero up to forty; Band B (middle, amber) running from forty up to ninety-five; and Band C (top, emerald) running from ninety-five up to one hundred thirty! To extract the true value of ANY middle or upper segment in a stacked chart, you MUST subtract its bottom boundary from its top boundary! Calculate carefully! Band A equals forty minus zero equals FORTY! Band B equals ninety-five minus forty equals FIFTY-FIVE! Band C equals one hundred thirty minus ninety-five equals THIRTY-FIVE! Check the sum: forty plus fifty-five plus thirty-five equals ONE HUNDRED THIRTY—matching the overall column height cleanly! Always subtract bottom from top before reading any upper stacked segment, and verify that all extracted segment values sum back cleanly to the top boundary!
