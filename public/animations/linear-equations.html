Section 3.1 • Introduction

Real-Life Modeling: Akhila at the Village Fair

Akhila wants rides on the Giant Wheel (costs ₹3 per ride) and wants to play Hoopla (costs ₹4 per game). She spends exactly ₹20, and played Hoopla half as many times as she rode the Giant Wheel!

🎡
Class 10 Algebra
General Form: a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0

📝 Translating English to Mathematics

Let x = number of Giant Wheel rides.
Let y = number of Hoopla games played.
Condition 1: Hoopla is half of rides:
y = x / 2 ⟹ x - 2y = 0 (Equation 1)
Condition 2: Total cost is ₹20:
3x + 4y = 20 (Equation 2)
Standard Form

General Pair of Linear Equations

a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0
a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0

Where a₁, b₁, c₁, a₂, b₂, c₂ are real numbers, and a₁^2 + b₁^2 ≠ 0, a₂^2 + b₂^2 ≠ 0.

Solution: x = 4 rides & y = 2 games (Cost = 3(4) + 4(2) = 12 + 8 = ₹20!)
NCERT Section 3.2 • Table 3.1

The 3 Geometric Cases & Ratio Tests

By simply comparing the ratios (a₁) / (a₂), (b₁) / (b₂), (c₁) / (c₂), we can predict whether the lines intersect, coincide, or run parallel!

Case 1

Intersecting Lines

a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂

Lines cross at exactly 1 point.

Unique Solution • Consistent
Case 2

Coincident Lines

a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂

Lines lie directly on top of each other.

Infinitely Many • Dependent (Consistent)
Case 3

Parallel Lines

a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂

Lines never meet in the plane.

No Solution • Inconsistent

NCERT Section 3.3 • The Need for Algebra

Why Do We Need Algebraic Methods?

The graphical method fails when solutions are non-integers like (√3, 2√7), (-1.75, 3.3), or (4/13, 1/19)!

Step 1

Express One Variable

Pick either equation and write y in terms of x (or x in terms of y).

Step 2

Substitute into Other Equation

Replaces that variable, reducing to a single linear equation in 1 variable!

Step 3

Solve & Back-Substitute

Solve for the first variable, then plug it back to get the second variable.

Crucial Note on Variable-Free Results (NCERT Page 31)

True Statement (e.g. 18 = 18):

The pair has infinitely many solutions (coincident lines).

False Statement (e.g. -4 = 0):

The pair has no solution (parallel lines / rails).

Section 3.3.2 • Elimination Method

The Elimination Method (Equalising Coefficients)

Multiply equations by suitable constants to make the coefficients of one variable numerically equal, then add or subtract to eliminate that variable!

💰 NCERT Example 8: Income Ratio 9:7, Expenditure 4:3, Saving ₹2000
Equations formed:
9x - 4y = 2000 --- (1)
7x - 3y = 2000 --- (2)
Step 1: Match y coefficients
Multiply (1) by 3 ⟹ 27x - 12y = 6000
Multiply (2) by 4 ⟹ 28x - 12y = 8000
Step 2: Subtract to eliminate y
(28x - 27x) - (12y - 12y) = 8000 - 6000
⟹ x = 2000
Step 3: Solve for y & Incomes
9(2000) - 4y = 2000 ⟹ y = 4000
Incomes: ₹18,000 & ₹14,000!
NCERT Page 35 • Example 10

Two-Digit Number & Reversing Digits

The sum of a two-digit number and the number obtained by reversing its digits is 66. If the digits differ by 2, find the number!

Algebraic Formulation

Let ten's digit be x and unit's digit be y.

Original Number = 10x + y
Reversed Number = 10y + x
Sum is 66: (10x + y) + (10y + x) = 66
⟹ 11(x + y) = 66 ⟹ x + y = 6 --- (1)

Two Valid Solutions!

Case A: x - y = 2
Add to (1): 2x = 8 ⟹ x = 4, y = 2
Number = 42
Case B: y - x = 2
Add to (1): 2y = 8 ⟹ y = 4, x = 2
Number = 24
Verification: 42 + 24 = 66, and |4 - 2| = 2!
