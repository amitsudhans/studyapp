Paper Weaving • Row Instructions & Grid Patterns

Weaving Mats

Slide 1 of 20

Paper weaving combines spatial geometry, sequence algorithms, and structural tension through interlacing vertical slits (warp) and horizontal strips (weft).

📐 The Weaving Algorithm

  • • Warp (Vertical Base): Folded paper with parallel cuts forming fixed vertical bands.
  • • Weft (Horizontal Strips): Movable colored strips woven horizontally through the warp.
  • • Standard Alternating Weave:
    • Row 1: 1 Over, 1 Under, 1 Over, 1 Under...
    • Row 2: 1 Under, 1 Over, 1 Under, 1 Over...
    • Row 3: 1 Over, 1 Under, 1 Over, 1 Under...

🎨 Advanced Pattern Rules

  • • Twill Weave (2 Over, 1 Under): Shifting the starting point by 1 unit each row produces diagonal staircases and herringbone textures.
  • • Basket Weave (2 Over, 2 Under): Creates bold checkerboard blocks of paired colors.
  • • Structural Principle: Interlacing locks paper fibers perpendicularly, preventing the mat from unravelling.
Binary Matrix Insight: A woven mat can be represented as a mathematical grid of 1s (strip over) and 0s (strip under), where alternating rows satisfy the parity rule Cell(r, c) = (r + c) mod 2.

Exploring Shapes • Why Pentagons Fail & Multi-Shape Tilings

Polygon Tiling

Slide 3 of 20

Shapes whose interior angles do not evenly divide 360° cannot tile alone, but can form hybrid tessellations when paired with matching partner shapes.

❌ Why Regular Pentagons Fail

  • • Interior angle of regular pentagon = 108°.
  • • 3 pentagons at a vertex: 3 × 108° = 324°.
  • • Remaining gap = 360° - 324° = 36°.
  • • 4 pentagons: 4 × 108° = 432° > 360° (overlap!).
  • • Conclusion: 360 ÷ 108 = 3.33 (not a whole number). Pentagons cannot tile alone!

✓ Octagon-Square Hybrid Tiling

  • • Interior angle of regular octagon = 135°.
  • • 2 octagons at a vertex: 2 × 135° = 270°.
  • • Deficit angle to complete 360°: 360° - 270° = 90°.
  • • A square has 90° corners: 135° + 135° + 90° = 360°!
  • • Octagons and squares tile the plane together in perfect harmony.
Only Three Regular Monotilings: There are only three regular polygons that can tile the plane by themselves: the Equilateral Triangle, the Square, and the Regular Hexagon.

Classification by Sides & Angles • Rhombus Dissection

Triangle Types

Slide 4 of 20

Triangles are classified into three distinct families based on the equality of their side lengths and interior angles:

🔺 Equilateral Triangle

• Sides: All 3 equal (a = b = c)

• Angles: All 3 equal = 60°

• Symmetry: 3 lines of symmetry

• 60° + 60° + 60° = 180°

📐 Isosceles Triangle

• Sides: At least 2 equal

• Angles: 2 base angles equal

• Symmetry: 1 line of symmetry

• If sides equal, opp angles equal!

📏 Scalene Triangle

• Sides: All 3 different

• Angles: All 3 different

• Symmetry: 0 lines of symmetry

• Sum still equals 180°

Rhombus Dissection: Cutting a rhombus along its perpendicular diagonals divides it into 4 congruent right-angled scalene (or isosceles) triangles meeting at 90°.

Quadrilateral Properties • Adjacent Equal Sides & Diagonal Symmetry

Kite Geometry

Slide 5 of 20

A kite is a quadrilateral characterized by two pairs of equal-length adjacent sides and mutually perpendicular diagonals.

🪁 Side & Angle Properties

  • • Adjacent Equal Sides: Top pair AB = AD, bottom pair CB = CD.
  • • Opposite Sides: Are not equal (AB ≠ CD).
  • • One Pair of Equal Angles: The angles between the unequal sides are equal (∠ B = ∠ D).
  • • Line of Symmetry: The main diagonal AC is a line of reflective symmetry.

✛ Diagonal Relationships

  • • Perpendicular Diagonals: The two diagonals cross at a 90° right angle (AC ⊥ BD).
  • • Bisecting Property: The vertical diagonal bisects the horizontal cross-diagonal into two equal segments.
  • • Special Case (Rhombus): A rhombus is an equilateral kite where all 4 sides are equal!
Aerodynamic Geometry: The symmetry axis and perpendicular cross-struts provide maximum stability and wind resistance when flying in the sky.

Quadrilateral Taxonomy • Opposites, Rights Angles & Rhombuses

Parallelograms and Rectangles

Slide 6 of 20

The quadrilateral family tree connects parallelograms, rectangles, rhombuses, and squares through progressive angle and side constraints:

▱ Parallelogram vs Rectangle

  • • Parallelogram: Opposite sides parallel & equal; opposite angles equal; diagonals bisect each other.
  • • Rectangle: A parallelogram where all 4 angles = 90°.
  • • Diagonal Invariant: In a rectangle, the two diagonals are equal in length (d₁ = d₂).

◇ Rhombus vs Square

  • • Rhombus: A parallelogram where all 4 sides are equal; diagonals cross at 90°.
  • • Square: Combines rectangle and rhombus properties: 4 equal sides AND 4 right angles (90°)!
  • • Hierarchy: Every square is a rectangle, every square is a rhombus, and all are parallelograms.
Diagonal Diagnostic: Bisect each other ➔ Parallelogram; Equal length ➔ Rectangle; Perpendicular (90°) ➔ Rhombus or Kite; Both equal and perpendicular ➔ Square!

Tangram Puzzle • Geometric Dissection & Shape Synthesis

Seven Tangrams

Slide 7 of 20

The 7-piece tangram puzzle decomposes a unit square into proportional geometric tiles sharing 45°, 90°, and 135° angles:

🧩 The 7 Tan Pieces

  • • 2 Large Triangles: Each has area = 1/4 of total square.
  • • 1 Medium Triangle: Area = 1/8 of total square.
  • • 2 Small Triangles: Each area = 1/16 of total square.
  • • 1 Square: Area = 1/8 of total square.
  • • 1 Parallelogram: Area = 1/8 of total square.
  • • All 5 triangles are 45°-45°-90° isosceles triangles!

🔄 Equivalent Compositions

  • • 2 Small Triangles = 1 Square (hypotenuse joined).
  • • 2 Small Triangles = 1 Medium Triangle (legs joined).
  • • 2 Small Triangles = 1 Parallelogram (reversed legs joined).
  • • 2 Large Triangles + 1 Medium + 2 Small + 1 Square + 1 Parallelogram = 1 Big Whole Square!
Conservation of Area: No matter how the 7 pieces are rearranged (into a swan, a running person, or a house), the total surface area remains exactly equal to the original square.

Make Your Own Kite • Step-by-Step Origami Geometry

Folding Kites

Slide 9 of 20

Paper folding creates precise geometric forms through angle bisections and line reflections along a square's diagonal axis:

📄 The Origami Folding Sequence

  1. Start with a Square: 4 equal sides and 4 right angles (90°).
  2. Diagonal Crease: Fold diagonally corner-to-corner to form line of symmetry AC.
  3. Left Wing Fold: Fold top-left edge inward along diagonal AC.
  4. Right Wing Fold: Fold top-right edge symmetrically inward along diagonal AC.
  5. Result: A true geometric kite with 2 pairs of adjacent equal sides!

📐 Geometric Verifications

  • • Angle Bisection: The diagonal fold bisects the original 90° corner into two equal 45° angles.
  • • Congruent Triangles: The kite consists of two mirror-image right-angled triangles folded across the diagonal.
  • • Perpendicular Struts: Attaching horizontal bamboo cross-struts forms the 90° diagonal intersection.
Origami Axiom: Folding a straight edge onto another creates an exact angle bisector without using a protractor!

Play with Circles • Compass Construction & Inscribed Quadrilaterals

Circle Geometry

Slide 10 of 20

Diameters inscribed in a circle act as diagonals of geometric quadrilaterals whose vertices lie on the circumference:

⭕ Core Circle Metrics

  • • Centre (O): The unique point equidistant from all points on the circumference.
  • • Radius (r): Distance from centre to any boundary point.
  • • Diameter (d): Longest straight line passing through the centre: d = 2r.
  • • Chord: Any line segment connecting two points on the circle.

🟩 Inscribed Quadrilateral Theorem

  • • Case 1 (Non-Perpendicular Diameters): Any two diameters have equal length (d₁ = d₂) and bisect at O. Connecting endpoints yields a Rectangle!
  • • Case 2 (Perpendicular Diameters at 90°): The two equal diameters cross at 90°. Connecting endpoints yields a Square!
Thales' Theorem: Any angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle (90°). Because diagonals are diameters, all 4 corner angles must be 90°!

Spatial Reasoning • 11 Hexomino Nets & 3-D Cube Folding

Cube Nets

Slide 12 of 20

A cube net is a flat arrangement of six connected squares that can be folded along its edges to construct a hollow 3-D cube:

📦 Cube Metrics & Net Rules

  • • Faces: 6 identical congruent squares.
  • • Edges: 12 straight equal line segments.
  • • Vertices: 8 corner points where 3 faces meet.
  • • Exactly 11 Distinct Nets: Only 11 hexomino patterns can fold into a closed cube.
  • • Opposite Face Rule: In any linear strip of a net, opposite faces are separated by exactly 1 square.

❌ Illegal Net Configurations

  • • 6 in a line: Fails (faces overlap, leaving ends open).
  • • 2×2 block of 4 squares: Fails (cannot fold closed).
  • • Corner clusters: If 4 squares meet at a single vertex point, they cannot fold without colliding.
Standard Die Invariant: On standard gaming dice, numbers on opposite faces always sum to 7: 1 opposite 6, 2 opposite 5, and 3 opposite 4!

Icosahedron & Dodecahedron • Regular Polyhedra & Euler's Formula

Platonic Solids

Slide 15 of 20

The five Platonic solids are the only convex polyhedra with identical regular polygon faces meeting at identical vertices:

🔷 Icosahedron (20 Faces)

• Faces (F): 20 (Equilateral Triangles)

• Vertices (V): 12 (5 triangles at each)

• Edges (E): 30

Euler Check: V - E + F = 12 - 30 + 20 = 2 ✓

⬟ Dodecahedron (12 Faces)

• Faces (F): 12 (Regular Pentagons)

• Vertices (V): 20 (3 pentagons at each)

• Edges (E): 30

Euler Check: V - E + F = 20 - 30 + 12 = 2 ✓

Dual Polyhedra: The icosahedron and dodecahedron are geometric duals: swapping the centers of the icosahedron's 20 faces produces the 20 vertices of the dodecahedron!

Straw and Clay Models • Structural Rigidity & 3D Engineering

Skeletal Frames

Slide 16 of 20

Constructing skeletal models with straws (edges) and clay balls (vertices) reveals the principles of 3-D structural rigidity:

🏗️ Triangulation & Rigidity

  • • Square Frame (Wobbly): 4 straws + 4 clay balls deform into a rhombus under lateral shear force.
  • • Diagonal Bracing: Adding 1 diagonal straw locks the angles completely, creating two rigid triangles.
  • • Engineering Reality: High-voltage towers, roof trusses, and Ferris wheels rely on triangular bracing.

📦 Polyhedral Skeletal Recipes

  • • Cube: 12 straws (equal length) + 8 clay balls.
  • • Square Pyramid: 8 straws (4 base + 4 slant) + 5 clay balls.
  • • Tetrahedron: 6 straws + 4 clay balls (inherently rigid!).
  • • Octahedron: 12 straws + 6 clay balls.
Rigidity Principle: A triangle is rigid because its three side lengths uniquely fix all three interior angles. No angle can change without altering the side lengths!
