Visible Surface Rule

Section 12.2 Core Law: Hidden Joined Faces Disappear, Only Exposed Surfaces Count Toward TSA

Slide 2 of 20
👨‍🏫
Classroom Teacher Spoken Lecture Pure Indian Male Teacher Sound

Master the SINGLE most important rule of Section Twelve Point Two right now, and you will NEVER get trapped on composite surface area problems! Whenever two or more basic solids are glued together face to face, what happens to the flat circular or square faces that touch each other inside? They become HIDDEN inside the solid! Because you can no longer touch or paint those glued interior faces from the outside, they DISAPPEAR completely from the Total Surface Area! For example, take a medicine test tube built from one cylinder and one bottom hemisphere of equal radius r! Never blindly add Cylinder TSA plus Hemisphere TSA! Instead, add ONLY the visible outside surfaces: Cylinder Curved Surface Area (2πrh) PLUS Top Open Rim (if closed) PLUS Hemisphere Curved Surface Area (2πr²)! Notice both internal base circles vanished cleanly! What if a hemisphere or cone is carved cleanly INSIDE a solid wooden block? You MUST SUBTRACT the flat circular disc opening that got scooped out, YET ADD the brand new exposed curved interior wall you can now touch directly inside the hollow cavity! Always pause carefully and ask yourself ONE golden rule: Where would exterior paint actually stick?

Welcome to Section Twelve Point Three: Volume of a Combination of Solids! Here comes the BEST news in geometry: unlike surface area where touching faces vanished cleanly inside, VOLUME NEVER DISAPPEARS when you join basic solids together!
