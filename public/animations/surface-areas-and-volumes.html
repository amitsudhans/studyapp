Section 12.1 • Foundations

Combinations of Solids: Beyond Single Shapes

In real life, engineering objects are rarely simple cubes or cylinders. Oil tankers, capsules, and rockets are composite solids formed by joining two or more basic shapes!

⚠️ Surface Area Law: Disappearing Faces!

When solids are glued together, their touching interfaces disappear inside! Never add TSAs. Only add the visible Curved Surface Areas (CSA).

✔️ Volume Law: Additive & Conserved!

Unlike surface area, capacity and matter are conserved: Total Volume is simply the direct sum of the individual volumes (V = V₁ + V₂).

Example 1 • Cone Mounted on Hemisphere

The Playing Top (Lattu): Rasheed's Birthday Gift

"A playing top is shaped like a cone surmounted by a hemisphere. Total height is 5 cm and diameter is 3.5 cm. Find the area to be coloured with crayons."

Fig. 12.6 Anatomy (Total Height = 5 cm)

Radius r = 3.5 / 2 = 1.75 cm
Height of cone h = 5 - 1.75 = 3.25 cm
Slant Height of Cone (l):
l = √(r² + h²) = √(1.75² + 3.25²) ≈ 3.7 cm
Total Area = CSA of Hemisphere + CSA of Cone
= 2πr² + πrl = πr(2r + l)
= (22 / 7) × 1.75 × (3.5 + 3.7) = 5.5 × 7.2
Surface Area = 39.6 cm² (approx.)
Area to Colour = 39.6 cm²
Example 2 • Cube with Hemispherical Mound/Cavity

Decorative Block: 6a² - πr² + 2πr² = 6a² + πr²

Whether a hemisphere is mounted on top of a cube OR scooped out as a depression, the net surface area formula is IDENTICAL!

Fig. 12.7 Cube (Edge 5 cm, Hemisphere d = 4.2 cm)

Surface Area Derivation:
TSA of Cube = 6 × a² = 6 × 5² = 150 cm²
The circular base of the hemisphere covers the cube top:
Subtract circular contact: - πr²
Add curved dome surface: + 2πr²
Net Surface Area = 150 + πr²
= 150 + (22 / 7) × 2.1 × 2.1 = 150 + 13.86
= 163.86 cm²
💡 Exercise 12.1 Q5 (Cavity scooped out): If hemisphere of diameter l is scooped out of a cube, the remaining surface area is also ¼ l²(24 + π)!
Medicine Capsule & Bird-Bath • Examples

Cylinder with Two Hemispheres: Capsules & Bird Baths

Standard shapes formed by adding or scooping hemispheres at the ends of a cylinder.

Ex 12.1 Q6: Capsule Total Length 14 mm

Diameter = 5 mm (r = 2.5 mm)

Length of Cylinder h = 14 - (2.5 + 2.5) = 9 mm
Surface Area = CSA of Cylinder + 2 × CSA of Hemisphere
= 2πrh + 2(2πr²) = 2πr(h + 2r)
= 2 × (22 / 7) × 2.5 × (9 + 5)
= 2 × (22 / 7) × 2.5 × 14 = 220 mm²
Example 4: Bird-Bath Height 1.45 m = 145 cm

Cylinder with Hemispherical Depression (r = 30 cm)

Surface Area = CSA of Cylinder + CSA of Hemisphere
= 2πrh + 2πr² = 2πr(h + r)
= 2 × (22 / 7) × 30 × (145 + 30)
= (1320 / 7) × 175 = 33,000 cm² = 3.3 m²
Example 5 • Volume of Combined Solids

Shanta's Industrial Shed: Cuboid + Half Cylinder Roof

"A shed in the shape of a cuboid (15m × 7m × 8m) surmounted by a half cylinder. Machinery occupies 300 m³ and 20 workers occupy 0.08 m³ each. How much air is inside?"

Fig. 12.12 Industrial Shed Structure

Cuboid Volume = 15 × 7 × 8 = 840 m³
Half-Cylinder r = 3.5 m, length = 15 m:
Vol = ½ × (22 / 7) × 3.5² × 15 = 288.75 m³
Total Empty Volume = 840 + 288.75 = 1128.75 m³
Subtract Space Occupied:
Machinery = 300 m³, Workers = 20 × 0.08 = 1.6 m³
Air Remaining = 1128.75 - 301.6 = 827.15 m³
Net Air in Shed = 827.15 m³
Example 6 • Apparent vs. Actual Capacity

The Juice Seller's Glass: Apparent vs. Actual Volume

"A cylindrical glass has diameter 5 cm and height 10 cm, but has a raised hemispherical bottom. Find its apparent capacity and actual capacity (π = 3.14)."

Fig. 12.13 Cross-Section

1. Apparent Capacity (Full Cylinder):
V_apparent = πr²h = 3.14 × 2.5² × 10 = 196.25 cm³
2. Volume of Hemispherical Base:
V_hemi = ⅔ πr³ = ⅔ × 3.14 × (2.5)³ = 32.71 cm³
3. Actual Liquid Capacity:
V_actual = 196.25 - 32.71 = 163.54 cm³
Apparent = 196.25 cm³ | Actual = 163.54 cm³
Example 7 • Enclosing Geometric Solids

Toy & Circumscribed Cylinder Volume Difference

"A solid toy has a hemisphere surmounted by a cone of height 2 cm and base diameter 4 cm. If a cylinder circumscribes the toy, find the difference in volumes."

Fig. 12.14 Cylinder Enclosing Cone + Hemisphere

Radius r = 2 cm, Cone Height h = 2 cm
1. Volume of Toy:
V_toy = ⅔ πr³ + ⅓ πr²h = ⅓ πr²(2r + h)
= ⅓ × 3.14 × 4 × (4 + 2) = 25.12 cm³
2. Volume of Circumscribing Cylinder:
Cylinder Height H = AO + OP = 2 + 2 = 4 cm
V_cyl = πr²H = 3.14 × 2² × 4 = 50.24 cm³
Difference = 50.24 - 25.12 = 25.12 cm³
Difference = 25.12 cm³ (Toy occupies exactly half the cylinder!)
Exercise 12.2 Q3 • Sweet Problem Solving

The 45 Gulab Jamuns Problem: 30% Sugar Syrup

"A gulab jamun contains sugar syrup up to 30% of its volume. Find syrup in 45 gulab jamuns, each shaped like a cylinder with two hemispherical ends of length 5 cm and diameter 2.8 cm."

Fig. 12.15 Single Gulab Jamun Model

Radius r = 2.8 / 2 = 1.4 cm
Cylinder Height h = 5 - 2(1.4) = 2.2 cm
Vol of 1 Gulab Jamun = πr²h + 4/3 πr³
= πr²(h + 4/3 r) = (22/7) × 1.96 × (2.2 + 1.87)
= 6.16 × 4.07 = 25.07 cm³
Total Volume of 45 Jamuns = 45 × 25.07 = 1128.15 cm³
Syrup Volume = 30% × 1128.15 ≈ 338 cm³
Total Sugar Syrup ≈ 338 cm³
Exercise 12.2 Q4 & Q5 • Cavity & Displacement

Wooden Pen Stand & Lead Shots Displacement

Subtracting conical cavities from a solid block of wood, and finding the count of spherical lead shots from water overflow!

Ex 12.2 Q4: Wooden Pen Stand

Cuboid (15 × 10 × 3.5) with 4 Conical Depressions

Cuboid Vol = 15 × 10 × 3.5 = 525 cm³
4 Cones (r = 0.5 cm, h = 1.4 cm):
Vol = 4 × ⅓ × (22 / 7) × (0.5)² × 1.4
= 4 × ⅓ × 22 × 0.25 × 0.2 = 1.47 cm³
Wood Volume = 525 - 1.47 = 523.53 cm³
Ex 12.2 Q5: Lead Shots Count

Inverted Cone (r = 5, h = 8) Filled with Water

Cone Vol = ⅓ π (5)² (8) = 200π / 3 cm³
Water overflow = ¼ × (200π / 3) = 50π / 3 cm³
Vol of 1 Lead Shot (r = 0.5):
4/3 π (0.5)³ = 0.5π / 3 cm³
Number of Shots = (50π / 3) / (0.5π / 3) = 100 shots!
