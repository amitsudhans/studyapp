Equal Sums

Balancing Number Groups with Minimal Swaps

⚖️

The Balancing Rule

To balance two groups with difference D in a single swap, find a number in the larger group that exceeds a number in the smaller group by exactly D / 2.

🎯

Target Equilibrium Sum

The balanced target sum is always the total combined sum of all cards divided by 2. Total Sum = Sum(A) + Sum(B); Target = Total / 2.

✈️

Real-World Balance

Aircraft luggage loaders and ship captains use this exact logic to distribute cargo evenly between left and right holds to ensure safe travel.

Addition with Regrouping

Place Value

Adding 2-Digit and 3-Digit Numbers with Column Regrouping

📦

The Regrouping Law

10 Ones bundle into 1 Ten (carries left); 10 Tens bundle into 1 Hundred; 10 Hundreds bundle into 1 Thousand.

📊

Strict Column Alignment

Always align digits from right to left starting at the Ones place: Ones under Ones, Tens under Tens, Hundreds under Hundreds.

✍️

Step-by-Step Addition

Step 1: Add Ones, carry over any 10s. Step 2: Add Tens including carried ten. Step 3: Add Hundreds including carried hundred.

Consecutive Numbers

Discovering Sums, Symmetries, and Middle-Number Multipliers

🔢

3 Consecutive Numbers Rule

For numbers (n-1), n, (n+1): Sum = 3 × Middle Number (n). Example: 10 + 11 + 12 = 3 × 11 = 33.

➕

4 Consecutive Numbers Rule

Sum of 4 consecutive numbers is always an even number! Example: 1+2+3+4 = 10; 2+3+4+5 = 14; 3+4+5+6 = 18. Each step increases by 4.

✨

Symmetry & Balance

Numbers equidistant from the centre pair up to equal sums. In 1, 2, 3, 4, 5: 1+5 = 6, 2+4 = 6, middle is 3. Total = 5 × 3 = 15.

Adding 9, 99, 999 (+100 - 1)

To add 99: Add 100, then subtract 1. Example: 456 + 99 = (456 + 100) - 1 = 556 - 1 = 555.

🔄

Subtracting 9, 99 (-100 + 1)

To add 198 + 45: Take 2 from 45 and add to 198 to create 200! Then 200 + 43 = 243 in seconds.

Number Pattern Wonder

Palindrome Numbers

Exploring Symmetrical Numbers That Read the Same Forwards and Backwards

🪞

Definition of Palindrome

A number that remains unchanged when its digits are reversed. Examples: 55, 121, 383, 2,332, 54,445.

🔢

3-Digit Palindrome Structure

A 3-digit palindrome has the form ABA, where the first and last digits (Hundreds and Ones) are identical (A != 0).

🪄

The Reverse-and-Add Trick

Start with a number: e.g. 52. Reverse digits: 25. Add them: 52 + 25 = 77! 77 is a palindrome.

📐

The Monotonic Grid Rule

For every cell (r, c): Cell(r, c) < Cell(r, c+1) (increasing across rows) and Cell(r, c) < Cell(r+1, c) (increasing down columns).

📌

Corner Invariants

Number Parity Principles

Even and Odd

Paired Dot Arrangements and the Laws of Sum Parity

👥

The Pairing Test

Even = Divisible by 2, zero remainder (complete pairs). Odd = Leaves a remainder of 1 when divided by 2 (one leftover item).

⚖️

The 3 Addition Parity Laws

1. Even + Even = Even (e.g. 6 + 8 = 14); 2. Odd + Odd = Even (e.g. 5 + 7 = 12); 3. Even + Odd = Odd (e.g. 4 + 7 = 11).

🔄

Consecutive Parity

Consecutive numbers always alternate: Even, Odd, Even, Odd... Therefore, adding any 2 consecutive numbers always gives an ODD sum (Even + Odd = Odd)!

Logic Puzzles

Mastering Coin Combinations and Torch Switch Parity

🪙

Coin Combination Analysis

To get ₹ 25 from ₹ 2 and ₹ 5 coins: 2a + 5b = 25. Since 2a is even and 25 is odd, 5b must be odd. Hence b must be odd (b = 1, 3, or 5).

🔦

Torch Switch Parity

Starting state: OFF. Press 1 (Odd) = ON; Press 2 (Even) = OFF; Press 3 (Odd) = ON... Press N: Odd N = ON, Even N = OFF.

💡

Binary State Logic

Two-state systems (ON/OFF, Open/Closed, Heads/Tails) obey modulo-2 arithmetic: State = Count % 2.
