Challenge 1

10 Questions Multi-Choice Quiz
1

A customer drinks 2 large tumblers of juice. If each tumbler holds the same as 3 small glasses, how many small glasses did they drink?

💡 Hint: Multiply 2 tumblers by 3 small glasses each.
2

Why is capacity measured by internal volume rather than just counting the number of cups?

💡 Hint: Think about whether a tiny espresso cup holds the same as a giant mug.
3

Three differently shaped glasses of milk are poured into three identical transparent jars. What tells us which glass held the most?

💡 Hint: In identical jars, a higher liquid line means greater volume.
4

It takes 3 small bowls of water to fill 1 drinking glass. How many bowls will it take to fill 4 drinking glasses?

💡 Hint: Multiply 4 glasses by 3 bowls per glass.
5

A lemonade jug can fill exactly 8 glasses. If 24 glasses of lemonade are needed for a party, how many jugs must be prepared?

💡 Hint: Divide 24 total glasses by 8 glasses per jug.
6

At a refreshment stall, 1 glass of lemonade requires 2 full ladles. How many ladles are needed for 7 glasses?

💡 Hint: Multiply 7 glasses by 2 ladles each.
7

A large mixing bowl holds 18 ladles of fruit punch. If each serving glass takes 3 ladles, how many glasses can be served from the bowl?

💡 Hint: Divide 18 ladles by 3 ladles per glass.
8

Which of these everyday containers usually has the SMALLEST liquid capacity?

💡 Hint: Think about which vessel delivers tiny drops of liquid.
9

If 5 glasses of water fill 1 bottle, and 4 bottles fill 1 bucket, how many glasses fill the bucket?

💡 Hint: Multiply glasses per bottle by bottles per bucket.
10

A short, wide mug and a tall, narrow glass both hold 300 mL of water. How does their capacity compare?

💡 Hint: Compare the numerical volume: 300 mL vs 300 mL.

What is the standard base unit of liquid capacity in the international metric system?

How many millilitres (mL) are contained in exactly 1 standard litre?

What is the volume of one-quarter of a litre (1/4 L) in millilitres?

Challenge 3

10 Questions Multi-Choice Quiz
1

What invisible natural force pulls heavier objects downward with greater force?

💡 Hint: It is the downward pull that gives objects weight on Earth.
2

On a two-pan balance, the left pan sinks downward while the right pan rises. What does this show?

💡 Hint: The heavier side always tilts downward.
3

A pencil box balances with 15 identical five-rupee coins. A pair of scissors balances with 9 identical coins. Which item is lighter?

💡 Hint: Fewer balancing coins mean the object is lighter.
4

Why must all units (like coins or erasers) used on a balance be identical in weight?

💡 Hint: Consistent units are the foundation of accurate measurement.
5

On a pan balance: Apple > Orange, and Orange > Plum. What is the order from lightest to heaviest?

💡 Hint: Plum is lighter than Orange, which is lighter than Apple.
6

A matchbox filled with dry sand balances with 5 metal paperclips. If a toy car balances with 3 sand matchboxes, how many paperclips equal the car?

💡 Hint: Multiply 3 matchboxes by 5 paperclips per matchbox.
7

When both pans of a balance are loaded and the central beam is completely level, what is this state called?

💡 Hint: Level beam indicates balanced, equal forces.
8

Which of these pairs of objects can be most easily compared by hand without a mechanical scale?

💡 Hint: Look for the pair with the most dramatic, obvious weight contrast.
9

A student places 4 marbles in the left pan and 6 marbles in the right pan. All marbles are identical. What happens?

💡 Hint: More identical marbles mean more weight on that side.
10

If an empty plastic cup is placed in the left pan and a cup full of sand is placed in the right pan, which side sinks?

💡 Hint: Sand adds substantial mass and weight to the cup.
