📜 The Etymology & Journey of "Sine" and "Cosine"

Sanskrit (500 CE) Aryabhata used ardha-jya (half-chord), shortened to jya or jiva.
Arabic Translation Retained as jiba in Arabic astronomical tables.
Latin Translation Translated into sinus (meaning fold or curve).
Modern Notation Edmund Gunter (1626) coined abbreviation sin; Jonas Moore (1674) coined cos.

Examples 3, 4 & 5 • Core Board Exam Solved Problems

Solving Right Triangles & Algebraic Verifications

Examining three high-frequency NCERT problems showing how trigonometric ratios interact with quadratic expansions and the Pythagorean theorem.

Example 3: Triangle (29, 21, θ)

Evaluate cos²θ + sin²θ

AC = √(29² - 21²) = √(50 × 8) = 20
sin θ = 20 / 29, cos θ = 21 / 29
cos²θ + sin²θ = (441+400)/841 = 1
cos²θ - sin²θ = (441-400)/841 = 41 / 841

Direct numerical verification of the universal identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1!

Example 4: 2 sin A cos A

If tan A = 1, Verify 2 sin A cos A = 1

tan A = BC / AB = 1 ➔ BC = AB = k
AC = √(k² + k²) = k√2
sin A = 1 / √2, cos A = 1 / √2
2 sin A cos A = 2 × (1/√2) × (1/√2) = 1

Verified! This is also the fundamental double-angle identity preview: sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A.

Example 5: Side Difference Trap

OP = 7 cm, OQ - PQ = 1 cm

OQ² = OP² + PQ²
(1 + PQ)² = 7² + PQ²
1 + 2PQ + PQ² = 49 + PQ²
2PQ = 48 ➔ PQ = 24 cm, OQ = 25 cm
sin Q = 7 / 25, cos Q = 24 / 25

Classic (7, 24, 25) Pythagorean triplet solved algebraically without guessing!

Section 8.3 • Geometric Derivations

Trigonometric Ratios of 45°, 30° & 60°

Never memorize blindly! Learn how these exact values arise geometrically from an isosceles right triangle and an equilateral triangle.

1. Derivation for 45° (Fig. 8.14)

AB = BC = a

In right triangle ABC with ∠A = 45°, ∠C is also 45°. Therefore AB = BC = a. By Pythagoras: AC = √(a² + a²) = a√2.

sin 45° 1 / √2
cos 45° 1 / √2
tan 45° 1
Reciprocals: cosec 45° = √2, sec 45° = √2, cot 45° = 1.

2. Derivation for 30° & 60° (Fig. 8.15)

Side = 2a

Equilateral triangle with side 2a. Altitude AD bisects base BC (BD = a) and bisects ∠A (∠BAD = 30°, ∠B = 60°). By Pythagoras: AD = √[(2a)² - a²] = a√3.

Ratios of 30°:
sin 30° = 1 / 2
cos 30° = √3 / 2
tan 30° = 1 / √3
Ratios of 60°:
sin 60° = √3 / 2
cos 60° = 1 / 2
tan 60° = √3
Limits & Continuity • Figs 8.17 & 8.18

Understanding Boundary Angles: 0° and 90°

What happens when an angle shrinks to 0° or expands to 90°? Visualizing geometric limits explains why some ratios become zero, one, or "not defined"!

📉 When ∠A Shrinks to 0° (Fig. 8.17)

As ∠A gets smaller and smaller, opposite side BC collapses toward 0, and hypotenuse AC almost coincides with adjacent AB:

• sin 0° = BC / AC ➔ 0 / AC = 0
• cos 0° = AB / AC ➔ AC / AC = 1
• tan 0° = sin 0° / cos 0° = 0 / 1 = 0
• sec 0° = 1 / cos 0° = 1
• cosec 0° = 1 / 0 ➔ Not Defined (Undefined)
• cot 0° = 1 / tan 0° ➔ Not Defined (Undefined)

📈 When ∠A Expands to 90° (Fig. 8.18)

As ∠A approaches 90°, ∠C shrinks to 0°. Adjacent side AB collapses toward 0, and hypotenuse AC almost coincides with opposite BC:

• sin 90° = BC / AC ➔ AC / AC = 1
• cos 90° = AB / AC ➔ 0 / AC = 0
• cot 90° = cos 90° / sin 90° = 0 / 1 = 0
• cosec 90° = 1 / sin 90° = 1
• tan 90° = sin 90° / cos 90° = 1 / 0 ➔ Not Defined
• sec 90° = 1 / cos 90° = 1 / 0 ➔ Not Defined

Section 8.4 • The 3 Pillar Identities

The 3 Fundamental Trigonometric Identities

Starting from Pythagoras Theorem AB² + BC² = AC², dividing by each of the three sides generates the three grand identities!

Divide by AC²

cos²A + sin²A = 1

(AB/AC)² + (BC/AC)² = (AC/AC)²
(cos A)² + (sin A)² = 1
Rearrangements:
• sin²A = 1 - cos²A
• cos²A = 1 - sin²A
Valid for: 0° ≤ A ≤ 90°
Divide by AB²

1 + tan²A = sec²A

1 + (BC/AB)² = (AC/AB)²
1 + tan²A = sec²A
Rearrangements:
• sec²A - tan²A = 1
• (sec A - tan A)(sec A + tan A) = 1
Valid for: 0° ≤ A < 90°
Divide by BC²

cot²A + 1 = cosec²A

(AB/BC)² + 1 = (AC/BC)²
cot²A + 1 = cosec²A
Rearrangements:
• cosec²A - cot²A = 1
• (cosec A - cot A)(cosec A + cot A) = 1
Valid for: 0° < A ≤ 90°
High-Yield Proofs • Board Exam Techniques

Proving Trigonometric Identities: The Winning Playbook

Master the standard proof heuristics: convert to sin and cos, multiply by conjugates, or divide by cos to trigger sec²θ - tan²θ = 1!

Example 10: sec A (1 - sin A)(sec A + tan A) = 1
LHS = (1/cos A) (1 - sin A) (1/cos A + sin A/cos A)
= (1 - sin A) (1 + sin A) / cos²A
= (1 - sin²A) / cos²A
= cos²A / cos²A = 1 = RHS! ✔️

Strategy: Turn everything into sines and cosines, then apply difference of squares.

Ex 8.3 Q4(viii): (sin+cosec)² + (cos+sec)² = 7 + tan² + cot²
LHS = (sin²A + 2·sin·cosec + cosec²A) + (cos²A + 2·cos·sec + sec²A)
= (sin²A + cos²A) + 2(1) + 2(1) + cosec²A + sec²A
= 1 + 4 + (1 + cot²A) + (1 + tan²A)
= 7 + tan²A + cot²A = RHS! ✔️

Strategy: Expand binomials and recognize reciprocal products sin A × cosec A = 1!
