Section 7.1 • Introduction

The Cartesian Plane: Connecting Algebra with Geometry

Coordinate geometry, pioneered by René Descartes, bridges algebra and geometry. It allows geometric figures to be analyzed algebraically and algebraic equations to be visualized geometrically.

📐
Descartes' Bridge
GPS, Physics & Computer Graphics

Abscissa (x-coordinate)

The perpendicular distance of a point from the y-axis. Points on the x-axis always have the coordinate format (x, 0).

Ordinate (y-coordinate)

The perpendicular distance of a point from the x-axis. Points on the y-axis always have the coordinate format (0, y).

Origin O(0, 0) & Curves

The axes intersect at O(0, 0). Linear equations ax+by+c=0 form straight lines; quadratics y=ax²+bx+c form parabolas!

How do we calculate distances without physical measurement? Consider Town B located 36 km East and 15 km North of Town A.

Theorem & Derivation • Section 7.2

Derivation of the Distance Formula

Finding the straight-line Euclidean distance between any two arbitrary points P(x₁, y₁) and Q(x₂, y₂) using the Pythagorean Theorem.

Proof Geometry (Fig. 7.5)

Δ PTQ is Right-Angled at T
⚡

Step-by-Step Proof

  1. Draw PR ⊥ x-axis and QS ⊥ x-axis.
  2. Draw PT ⊥ QS to create right-angled Δ PTQ.
  3. PT = RS = OS - OR = x₂ - x₁
  4. QT = SQ - ST = y₂ - y₁
  5. By Pythagoras Theorem in right triangle Δ PTQ:
PQ² = PT² + QT²
PQ² = (x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²
The Distance Formula
PQ = √[ (x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)² ]
Crucial Note: Since distance is always non-negative, we always take the positive square root!
Distance from origin O(0, 0) to P(x, y): OP = √(x² + y²)
Examples 1 & 2 • Geometric Proofs

Identifying Geometric Shapes with Distance

By finding side lengths and diagonals, we can decisively classify triangles (scalene, isosceles, equilateral, right-angled) and quadrilaterals (square, rhombus, rectangle, parallelogram).

Textbook Example 1 P(3,2), Q(-2,-3), R(2,3)

Do these points form a triangle? What type?

PQ = √[(3+2)² + (2+3)²] = √50 ≈ 7.07
QR = √[(-2-2)² + (-3-3)²] = √52 ≈ 7.21
PR = √[(3-2)² + (2-3)²] = √2 ≈ 1.41
✔️ Triangle Inequality: Sum of any two sides > third side:
PQ + PR = 7.07 + 1.41 = 8.48 > 7.21 (QR)
🎯 Converse of Pythagoras:
PQ² + PR² = 50 + 2 = 52 = QR²
Therefore, Δ PQR is a Right-Angled Triangle with hypotenuse QR and right angle at P!
Textbook Example 2 A(1,7), B(4,2), C(-1,-1), D(-4,4)

Show that ABCD is a Square

All 4 Sides:
AB = √34
BC = √34
CD = √34
DA = √34
Both Diagonals:
AC = √68
BD = √68
Quadrilateral Decision Rule:
  • • Square: 4 sides equal AND both diagonals equal (AC = BD).
  • • Rhombus: 4 sides equal BUT diagonals unequal.
  • • Rectangle: Opposite sides equal AND diagonals equal.
  • • Parallelogram: Opposite sides equal BUT diagonals unequal.

Collinear Points & Equidistant Loci

Are Ashima, Bharti & Camella in a Line?

Seated at A(3, 1), B(6, 4), C(8, 6)

Example 4: Perpendicular Bisector

Point P(x, y) Equidistant from A(7, 1) & B(3, 5)

AP² = BP²
(x - 7)² + (y - 1)² = (x - 3)² + (y - 5)²
Expand & cancel x², y² terms:
-14x + 50 - 2y = -6x + 34 - 10y
-8x + 8y = -16
x - y = 2

This linear equation is the perpendicular bisector of line segment AB!

Section 7.3 • Internal Division

The Section Formula: Proof by AA Similarity

Finding the coordinates of point P(x, y) dividing the line segment between A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂) internally in the ratio m₁ : m₂.

Similar Triangles Setup (Fig. 7.10)

By AA similarity: Δ PAQ ~ Δ BPC
Ratio of Proportional Sides:
PA / BP = AQ / PC = PQ / BC
m₁ / m₂ = (x - x₁) / (x₂ - x) = (y - y₁) / (y₂ - y)

Cross-multiplying m₁(x₂ - x) = m₂(x - x₁) isolates x, and identically isolates y:

The Section Formula (Internal Division)
P(x, y) = ( m₁x₂ + m₂x₁ / m₁ + m₂ , m₁y₂ + m₂y₁ / m₁ + m₂ )
The k : 1 Shortcut: When finding an unknown ratio, let m₁/m₂ = k : 1:
P = ( (k x₂ + x₁) / (k + 1) , (k y₂ + y₁) / (k + 1) )
Special Cases • Trisection & Midpoint

The Mid-Point Formula & Points of Trisection

When a line is divided equally (1 : 1), we obtain the famous Mid-Point Formula. When divided into three equal parts (1 : 2 and 2 : 1), we obtain Points of Trisection.

1. The Mid-Point Formula (1 : 1)

m₁ = m₂ = 1

The mid-point M divides the line segment joining A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂) exactly in half:

M = ( (x₁ + x₂) / 2 , (y₁ + y₂) / 2 )

• Q divides in 2 : 1 ➔ ( [2(-7)+1(2)]/3 , [2(4)+1(-2)]/3 ) = (-4, 2)

Examples 7, 9 & 10 • Advanced Techniques

Finding Unknown Ratios & Parallelogram Diagonals

Essential board exam questions: determining the ratio in which points and axes divide segments, and solving vertices using parallelogram diagonal properties.

Example 7: Unknown Ratio

In what ratio does (-4, 6) divide A(-6, 10) & B(3, -8)?

Let ratio be k : 1
x = (3k - 6) / (k + 1) = -4
-4k - 4 = 3k - 6
7k = 2 ➔ k = 2/7
Ratio = 2 : 7
Check y: [-8(2/7)+10]/(2/7+1) = 54/9 = 6. Verified!
Example 9: Division by Axis

Ratio in which y-axis divides (5, -6) & (-1, -4)

On y-axis, x-coordinate is 0!
Let ratio be k : 1
x = [k(-1) + 5] / (k + 1) = 0
-k + 5 = 0 ➔ k = 5
y = [-4(5) - 6] / 6 = -26/6 = -13/3
Ratio = 5 : 1
Point = (0, -13/3)
Example 10: Missing Vertex p

A(6,1), B(8,2), C(9,4), D(p,3) are Parallelogram Vertices

Golden Property: Diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other!

Midpoint AC = ( (6+9)/2 , (1+4)/2 ) = (15/2 , 5/2)
Midpoint BD = ( (8+p)/2 , (2+3)/2 ) = ( (8+p)/2 , 5/2 )
15/2 = (8 + p)/2
15 = 8 + p ➔ p = 7
Value of p = 7
