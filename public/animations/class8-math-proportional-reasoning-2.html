Direct Versus Inverse

Welcome to the centerpiece of Chapter Ten—Section Three Point Six: INVERSE PROPORTIONS! Until now, every proportion scaled quantities TOGETHER in the same direction: double recipe flour, double recipe sugar—keeping their QUOTIENT constant (x/y = k), known as DIRECT PROPORTION! Now discover the opposite mathematical universe! What happens when INCREASING one quantity causes its partner quantity to DECREASE proportionately? Suppose a fixed amount of physical work waits to be finished, or a fixed road distance waits to be driven: hiring TWICE as many painters HALVES painting time; tripling driving speed cuts travel duration cleanly down to ONE-THIRD! Whenever multiplying variable x by scaling factor n forces partner variable y to multiply by reciprocal factor 1/n, x and y stand in INVERSE PROPORTION! Spot the crystal-clear algebraic signature distinguishing both mathematical worlds! In DIRECT proportion, the QUOTIENT stays locked constant forever: x/y = k (equivalently x₁/y₁ = x₂/y₂)! In INVERSE proportion, the PRODUCT stays locked constant forever: x · y = k (equivalently x₁ y₁ = x₂ y₂)! Checking carefully whether row products stay completely equal versus row quotients across numerical data tables immediately reveals which proportionality law truly governs every single quantitative real-world problem and data table!
