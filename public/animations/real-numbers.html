Section 1.1 • Introduction

The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every natural number can be written as a product of prime numbers in an essentially unique way.

👑
Carl Friedrich Gauss (1777–1855)
Prince of Mathematicians • Disquisitiones Arithmeticae
Concept 1

Prime Numbers (Building Blocks)

Numbers greater than 1 whose only divisors are 1 and themselves.

Composite Numbers

Numbers that can be broken into products of smaller primes.

Uniqueness of Factorization

Apart from the order of factors, there is only one way to write any composite number as primes!

HCF & LCM by Prime Factorisation

Fundamental Identity: HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b

Product of the smallest powers of each common prime factor.

Example 1 & Exercise 1.1

Can 4ⁿ or 6ⁿ Ever End with Digit 0?

Applying the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic to prove impossible ending digits.

Case 1: 4ⁿ

NEVER 0
1. To end in 0, prime 5 must be a factor (2 × 5 = 10).
2. Factor 4ⁿ: 4ⁿ = (2²)ⁿ = 2²ⁿ
3. The only prime factor is 2!
Conclusion: Since 5 does not occur in 2²ⁿ, 4ⁿ cannot end in 0 for any natural number n.

Case 2: 6ⁿ

NEVER 0
1. Any number ending in 0 is divisible by 5.
2. Factor 6ⁿ: 6ⁿ = (2 × 3)ⁿ = 2ⁿ × 3ⁿ
3. The only primes present are 2 and 3.
Uniqueness Theorem: No primes other than 2 and 3 can exist. Hence, 6ⁿ can NEVER end in 0.

Exercise 1.1 Question 7

Circular Sports Track Simulation

Sonia takes 18 min/lap, Ravi takes 12 min/lap. When will they meet at the start?

🔴 Sonia (18 min) 🔵 Ravi (12 min) Time: 0.0 min
Mathematical Solution:

They meet at common multiples of 18 and 12 minutes:

Sonia (18): 18, 36, 54, 72...
Ravi (12): 12, 24, 36, 48...
LCM(18, 12) = 2² × 3² = 36 min

Sonia completes exactly 2 laps (2 × 18 = 36) and Ravi completes 3 laps (3 × 12 = 36) when they meet!
