We know √2 cannot end as a terminating decimal—could it perhaps equal a clean repeating fraction like m/n? Around three hundred BCE in his Elements, Euclid delivered one of history's most celebrated proofs showing √2 is completely IRRATIONAL—impossible to express as ANY integer ratio m/n! Follow Euclid's stunning prime-factor parity proof step by step! Suppose, just for argument, that positive integers m and n satisfy √2 = m/n! Square both sides cleanly to eliminate the root: 2 = m²/n², then multiply by n² to obtain the master integer equation: 2n² = m²! Now count how many times prime factor 2 divides each side! In ANY perfect square like m² or n², every prime factor of the base appears TWICE—meaning EVERY square holds an EVEN number of factor 2s (0, 2, 4, 6, and so on)! Look at the right-hand side m²: EVEN count of 2s! Now look at the left-hand side 2n²: n² has an EVEN count of 2s, PLUS ONE extra explicit factor of 2 in front! Even plus one equals ODD! One side has an ODD number of 2s while the identical number has an EVEN number—contradiction! Hence √2 can NEVER equal m/n!
