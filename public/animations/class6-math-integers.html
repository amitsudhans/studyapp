Slide 1 of 20 Intro • Number Systems

Number Evolution

From Counting Sheaves to the Infinite Two-Sided Line

Humanity began with natural counting numbers: 1, 2, 3, 4, ... to count animals, trees, and coins. Later, zero (0) arrived to represent an empty basket or the absence of items, giving us Whole Numbers (0, 1, 2, 3, ...).

To share chikkis and measure fractional lengths, we created Fractions (1/2, 3/4, 2/5). These all rested on a one-directional Number Ray pointing to the right starting from 0.

What lies to the left of zero? If you owe money, or dive beneath sea level, or take an elevator underground, we need numbers less than zero! Extending the ray symmetrically to the left completes the infinite, two-sided Number Line.

Slide 3 of 20 10.1 • Definitions

Signs & Direction

Positive (+), Negative (−), and the Neutral Origin Zero

Numbers with a plus sign (+) are called Positive Numbers (+1, +2, +3, ...). When writing positive numbers, the plus sign is often omitted (so 5 means +5).

Numbers with a minus sign (−) are called Negative Numbers (−1, −2, −3, ...). The minus sign is mandatory!

Zero (0) is neither positive nor negative. It acts as the grand mirror pivot and dividing origin between both realms.

Together, all negative whole numbers, zero, and positive whole numbers form the set of Integers: {..., −3, −2, −1, 0, +1, +2, +3, ...}.

Slide 4 of 20 10.1 • Addition

Tracking Movement

Starting Floor + Movement = Target Floor

When Bela rides the elevator, upward movement is represented by adding a positive integer, while downward movement is represented by adding a negative integer.

The fundamental motion equation is:
Starting Floor + Movement = Target Floor

For instance, if Bela starts at Floor +2 and moves down 3 floors: (+2) + (−3) = −1. She lands on the first basement level!

Similarly, starting at Floor −2 and moving up 5 floors: (−2) + (+5) = +3.

Slide 5 of 20 10.1 • Additive Inverses

Additive Inverses

Opposite Pairs that Cancel Exactly Back to Zero

What happens if Bela goes up 3 floors from the ground (+3) and then immediately goes down 3 floors (−3)?

(+3) + (−3) = 0

Two numbers whose sum is zero are called Additive Inverses (or opposites) of each other. For every positive integer +a, there exists an exact negative twin −a such that:
(+a) + (−a) = 0

The additive inverse of +7 is −7. The additive inverse of −12 is +12. And the additive inverse of 0 is simply 0!

Slide 6 of 20 10.1 • Ordering

Comparing Depths

Higher Floors Are Greater; Lower Floors Are Smaller

In Bela's tower, higher floors are physically higher up, and lower floors are deeper underground. In mathematics, this vertical ordering defines the inequality relationship:

  • Any positive number is greater than 0: +5 > 0.
  • Any negative number is smaller than 0: −3 < 0.
  • Every positive number is greater than every negative number: +1 > −100!
  • Between two negative numbers, the one deeper underground is smaller: −5 < −2.

Think of temperature or depth: −10 °C is much colder (lower) than −2 °C!

Slide 7 of 20 10.1 • Subtraction

Finding the Shift

Target Floor − Starting Floor = Movement Needed

Instead of thinking of subtraction merely as 'taking away', integer subtraction asks: 'What movement is required to travel from a starting floor to a target floor?'

Target Floor − Starting Floor = Movement Needed

Suppose Bela is on Floor −2 and wants to reach Floor +3. What button must she press?
(+3) − (−2) = +5 (she must move UP 5 floors!).

Suppose she is on Floor +4 and wants to reach Floor −1:
(−1) − (+4) = −5 (she must move DOWN 5 floors!).

Slide 9 of 20 10.1 • Core Law

Double Negatives

Subtracting a Negative Number is Adding a Positive

One of the most powerful laws in mathematics is:
Subtracting a negative number is exactly equivalent to adding its positive twin!

a − (−b) = a + b

Why does this make total sense?

  • In Banking: If the bank removes (−) a debt of 500 rupees (−500) from your account, your wealth increases by +500!
  • In Bela's Lift: Finding the movement from −200 up to +2000 is (+2000) − (−200) = 2000 + 200 = +2200 floors up.
  • Taking away cold makes things warmer!

Slide 10 of 20 10.1 • Number Line

Horizontal Shift

Rotating the Vertical Elevator 90° onto the Number Line

Take Bela's vertical elevator shaft and rotate it 90° counter-clockwise. What happens?

  • The ground floor (0) becomes the central Origin (0).
  • Upward positive floors (+1, +2, +3, ...) swing into the Right side.
  • Downward negative floors (−1, −2, −3, ...) swing into the Left side.

Thus, walking Right means moving in the positive direction (adding positive or subtracting negative). Walking Left means moving in the negative direction (adding negative or subtracting positive).

Slide 11 of 20 10.1 • Unmarked Line

The Unmarked Line

Mental Arithmetic with Relative Distance Arcs

When working with large numbers like (+45) + (−70) or (−120) − (−150), drawing dozens of individual tick marks is impractical.

Instead, mathematicians use an Unmarked Number Line with only 0 marked as an anchor.

To solve (−35) + (+50):

  1. Place −35 to the left of 0.
  2. Jump right by +35 to reach 0.
  3. You still have 50 − 35 = 15 left to jump to the right of 0.
  4. You land at +15!

Slide 12 of 20 10.2 • Tokens

The Token Model

Green Positive (+), Red Negative (−), and Zero Pairs

Integers can be physically visualized using colored circular counters:

+1 Token (Green) −1 Token (Red)

When one green token (+1) meets one red token (−1), they cancel each other out completely: (+1) + (−1) = 0. This pair is called a Zero Pair.

To find (+4) + (−2): take 4 green tokens and 2 red tokens. Pair up 2 green with 2 red (they vanish as zero pairs). You are left with 2 green tokens: +2!

Slide 13 of 20 10.2 • Zero Pairs

Token Subtraction

Injecting Zero Pairs When Tokens Are Missing

How do you compute (+2) − (−3) using tokens? You have 2 green tokens, but you need to take away 3 red tokens — and there are no red tokens on the table!

The brilliant secret: Add 3 Zero Pairs (3 green + 3 red). Since zero pairs equal 0, adding them does not change the total value of the set!

  • Initial state: 2 green (+2).
  • Add 3 zero pairs: Now you have (2 + 3 = 5 green) and 3 red. (Net value is still +2).
  • Now take away the 3 red tokens!
  • You are left with 5 green tokens: +5!

Slide 16 of 20 10.5 • History

Brahmagupta's Laws

Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta (628 CE) and Global Mathematical Heritage

In 628 CE, the legendary Indian astronomer-mathematician Acharya Brahmagupta composed the Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta, writing the world's first complete, rigorous rules for negative numbers (ṛṇa - debt), positive numbers (dhana - fortune), and zero (kha):

  • 'The sum of two fortunes is a fortune; the sum of two debts is a debt.' ((+a)+(+b)=+(a+b); (−a)+(−b)=−(a+b)).
  • 'The sum of a fortune and a debt is their difference.'
  • 'A debt subtracted from zero becomes a fortune.' (0 − (−a) = +a).
  • 'A fortune subtracted from zero becomes a debt.' (0 − (+a) = −a).

These profound Indian rules spread to Baghdad via Al-Khwarizmi, entered Europe centuries later through Fibonacci, and form the cornerstone of modern algebra today.
