Notice ONE annoying arithmetic hurdle in the Direct Method whenever Class Marks xᵢ and frequencies fᵢ become large decimal numbers like 47.5, 92.5, or 1,250! Multiplying large fᵢ times xᵢ takes long error-prone vertical multiplication! Let us crush that arithmetic immediately with Method Two: the Assumed Mean Method! Grouped Mode Formula

Section 13.3: Modal Class (Maximum Frequency f₁) & Mode = l + [(f₁ − f₀)/(2f₁ − f₀ − f₂)] × h

Slide 8 of 20
👨‍🏫
Classroom Teacher Spoken Lecture Pure Indian Male Teacher Sound

Now conquer Section Thirteen Point Three: Mode of Grouped Data! In ungrouped data, Mode was simply the number appearing most frequently! Inside grouped data, we cannot see single values, so we first locate the MODAL CLASS—the interval holding the MAXIMUM FREQUENCY! How do we estimate the exact peak Mode inside that winning Modal Class? Memorize this crisp five-symbol formula with crystal precision: Mode equals l PLUS bracket open (f₁ minus f₀) divided by (2 f₁ minus f₀ minus f₂) bracket close, multiplied by class size h! Decode every single symbol cleanly: l equals Lower Limit of the modal class; h equals class size; f₁ equals frequency of the MODAL class (1 = winner!); f₀ equals frequency of the PRECEDING class just before it (0 comes before 1!); and f₂ equals frequency of the SUCCEEDING class right after it (2 comes after 1!)! Test NCERT Example Five (Family sizes survey): maximum frequency f₁ = 8 sits neatly in class 3–5 (lower limit l = 3, width h = 2), preceding class frequency f₀ = 7, succeeding class frequency f₂ = 2! Compute 3 + [(8 − 7) / (16 − 7 − 2)] × 2 = 3 + 2/7 = exact 3.286 family members!

Choosing Central Measure

Section 13.4 Guidelines: Comparing Mean (Uses All Data), Median (Outlier-Proof) & Mode (Most Common)

Slide 14 of 20
👨‍🏫
Classroom Teacher Spoken Lecture Pure Indian Male Teacher Sound

Which statistical measure of central tendency should a professional data analyst choose in practical real life situations: Mean, Median, or Mode? Examine Section Thirteen Point Four's textbook decision guidelines carefully! Pick MEAN whenever numerical data values cluster fairly symmetrically around a center without extreme high or low outliers, because Mean incorporates EVERY single observation mathematically with balanced equal weight! However, watch one classic pitfall carefully: if ONE billionaire walks into a small meeting room of ten ordinary salaried workers, the arithmetic Mean salary skyrockets artificially upward while misrepresenting almost everyone present in the room! Pick MEDIAN whenever extreme outlier values heavily distort one tail of the dataset OR whenever grouped class intervals contain OPEN ENDS such as 'below 20' or '80 and above'! Because Median evaluates only cumulative fifty percent position around the middle class, extreme high or low tail values cannot pull it sideways! Pick MODE whenever you need the SINGLE MOST POPULAR repeating choice, such as manufacturing shoe sizes, readymade shirt collar sizes, or tracking peak customer vehicle traffic across busy intersections! Notice Mode even works effortlessly on non-numerical consumer preferences and qualitative surveys! Always examine dataset shape and practical purpose carefully first!

Unlock Section Thirteen Point Four's celebrated Empirical Relationship linking all THREE central tendency measures together cleanly in one compact equation: THREE MEDIAN EQUALS MODE PLUS TWO MEAN! Understand carefully what the word 'empirical' signals mathematically here: this equation is NOT a rigid exact algebraic identity for every possible artificial dataset, yet across countless real-world moderately skewed distributions, whenever ANY TWO measures are already known, this formula predicts the THIRD measure accurately!
