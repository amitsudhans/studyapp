Section 9.1 • Foundational Concepts

Heights and Distances: The Three Core Definitions

Trigonometry transforms surveying and architecture. By observing angles from an eye level, we calculate inaccessible heights and vast distances with simple right-triangle trigonometry.

🔭
The Theodolite
Precision angle measuring instrument

1. Line of Sight

The straight imaginary line drawn directly from the eye of the observer to the point on the object viewed by the observer.

2. Angle of Elevation (Fig. 9.2)

The angle formed by the line of sight with the horizontal level when the object is ABOVE the horizontal (we raise our head to look up).

3. Angle of Depression (Fig. 9.3)

The angle formed by the line of sight with the horizontal level when the object is BELOW the horizontal (we lower our head to look down).

⚡ The Golden Parallel Line Theorem:

Because the horizontal line at the top is parallel to the ground horizontal, Angle of Depression = Angle of Elevation by Alternate Interior Angles!

∠Depression = ∠Elevation

Sightline Geometry

Angle of Elevation (Looking Up)

The observer's head tilts upward. The horizontal baseline starts at the eye level, and the line of sight extends up to the summit of the tower or mountain.

Formula: tan θ = Height (BC) / Distance (AB)
Observer's Height AE Rule:

When the problem specifies the observer's height (e.g. 1.5 m boy or 1.2 m girl), total height = calculated height (BC) + observer height (AE)!

Examples 1, 2 & 3 • Single Right Triangles

Direct Heights: Towers, Electrician Ladders & Chimneys

In standard problems, one side and one acute angle are known. Choose the trigonometric ratio (sin, cos, or tan) that connects the known side with the required side!

Example 1: Tower (Fig. 9.4)

15 m Away at 60° Elevation

tan 60° = AB / 15
√3 = AB / 15
Height AB = 15√3 m
≈ 15 × 1.732 = 25.98 m

Straightforward tangent ratio problem: Opposite = Adjacent × tan 60°.

Example 2: 5m Pole (Fig. 9.5)

Reach 1.3 m Below Top at 60°

BD = 5 - 1.3 = 3.7 m
sin 60° = 3.7 / Ladder (BC)
BC = 3.7 × 2 / √3 = 4.28 m
cot 60° = Foot / 3.7
Foot = 3.7 / √3 = 2.14 m

Must subtract 1.3m clearance before setting up right triangle!

Example 3: Chimney (Fig. 9.6)

1.5 m Observer, 28.5 m Away, 45°

tan 45° = AE / DE
1 = AE / 28.5 ➔ AE = 28.5 m
Total Height = AE + Observer
Height = 28.5 + 1.5 = 30 m

At 45°, opposite equals adjacent! Add observer height for full structure.

Exercise 9.1 Q2 • Board Exam Evergreen

The Broken Tree Storm Problem

"A tree breaks due to a storm and the broken top bends so that it touches the ground making an angle of 30° with it. The distance from the foot to the touch point is 8 m. Find the height of the tree."

Geometric Model (Broken Part = Hypotenuse)

Total Tree Height = Standing Trunk + Broken Slanted Part

Height of tree = AB + AC

1. Find Standing Part (AB):
tan 30° = AB / BC = AB / 8
1/√3 = AB / 8 ➔ AB = 8 / √3 m
2. Find Broken Part (AC):
cos 30° = BC / AC = 8 / AC
√3/2 = 8 / AC ➔ AC = 16 / √3 m
Total Tree Height = AB + AC
= 8/√3 + 16/√3 = 24 / √3 m
= (24 × √3) / 3 = 8√3 m
Tree Height = 8√3 m ≈ 13.86 m
Example 4 & Exercise 9.1 Q7/Q8 • Two-Angle Overlap

Two Angles from Same Point: Building & Flagstaff

When observing a pedestal and statue, or a building and flagstaff from a single point P on the ground, solve the shared horizontal baseline first!

Fig. 9.7 Geometry (Shared Base AP)

Step 1: In Δ PAB (Lower Angle 30°)
tan 30° = AB / AP = 10 / AP
1/√3 = 10 / AP ➔ AP = 10√3 m ≈ 17.32 m
Step 2: In Δ PAD (Upper Angle 45°)
tan 45° = AD / AP = (10 + x) / 10√3
1 = (10 + x) / 10√3
10 + x = 10√3
x = 10(√3 - 1) = 10(1.732 - 1) = 7.32 m
Distance to Building: 17.32 m Flagstaff Length: 7.32 m
Example 5 • Shifted Baselines

Shadow Length & The Sun's Altitude Change

"The shadow of a tower is found to be 40 m longer when the Sun's altitude is 30° than when it is 60°. Find the height of the tower."

Fig. 9.8 Geometry (Shadow Shift of 40m)

Simultaneous Triangle Equations:
From Δ ABC: tan 60° = h / x ➔ h = x√3 (1)
From Δ ABD: tan 30° = h / (x + 40) = 1 / √3 (2)
Substitute (1) into (2):
(x√3) × √3 = x + 40
3x = x + 40 ➔ 2x = 40 ➔ x = 20 m
h = 20√3 m ≈ 34.64 m
Tower Height = 20√3 m ≈ 34.64 m
Example 6 • Double Angles of Depression

Depression from a Multi-Storeyed Building

"The angles of depression of the top and bottom of an 8 m tall building from the top of a multi-storeyed building are 30° and 45°, respectively. Find the height of the multi-storeyed building and distance between them."

Fig. 9.9 Structure

In Δ PAC: tan 45° = PC / AC = 1 ➔ AC = PC
Since DC = AB = 8m, PC = PD + 8
In Δ PBD: tan 30° = PD / BD = 1 / √3
BD = PD√3, and BD = AC = PC
Equating PC expressions:
PD + 8 = PD√3 ➔ PD(√3 - 1) = 8
PD = 8 / (√3 - 1) = 4(√3 + 1) m
Total Height PC = 4(√3 + 1) + 8 = 4(3 + √3) m
Height = Distance = 4(3 + √3) m ≈ 18.93 m
Example 7 • Opposing Banks

Width of a River from a Bridge Point

"From a point on a bridge across a river, the angles of depression of opposite banks are 30° and 45°. If the bridge is at a height of 3 m, find the width of the river."

Fig. 9.10 River & Bridge Geometry

River Width = Left Segment (AD) + Right Segment (DB)
In right Δ APD: tan 30° = PD / AD
1 / √3 = 3 / AD ➔ AD = 3√3 m
In right Δ PBD: tan 45° = PD / DB
1 = 3 / DB ➔ DB = 3 m
Total River Width = AD + DB = 3√3 + 3 = 3(√3 + 1) m
Width of River = 3(√3 + 1) m ≈ 8.20 m
Exercise 9.1 Q14 & Q15 • Advanced Motion Modeling

Time, Speed & Horizontal Motion Problems

Applying trigonometry when objects move over time: balloons drifting with the wind, and cars speeding toward a tower foot.

Exercise 9.1 Q14: 88.2m Balloon

1.2 m Girl Spots Balloon Shifting 60° to 30°

Effective Height = 88.2 - 1.2 = 87 m
Position 1: tan 60° = 87 / d₁ ➔ d₁ = 87 / √3 m
Position 2: tan 30° = 87 / d₂ ➔ d₂ = 87√3 m
Distance Travelled = d₂ - d₁
= 87√3 - 87/√3 = 87(3 - 1)/√3
= (87 × 2)/√3 = 58√3 m ≈ 100.46 m

Subtract observer height 1.2m first, then subtract horizontal projections!

Exercise 9.1 Q15: Car Uniform Speed

Depression 30° to 60° in 6 Seconds

Let tower height be h, car speed be v.
Distance in 6 seconds: CD = 6v
In Δ ABC: tan 60° = h / BC ➔ h = BC√3
In Δ ABD: tan 30° = h / (BC + 6v) = 1 / √3
3·BC = BC + 6v ➔ 2·BC = 6v ➔ BC = 3v
Time = Distance / Speed = 3v / v = 3 seconds!

The remaining distance is exactly half the previously travelled distance, taking exactly 3 seconds!
