Now define a univariate POLYNOMIAL and classify it sharply by its degree! A univariate polynomial is an algebraic expression formed by combining constants and powers of a single variable using ONLY addition, subtraction, and multiplication—crucially requiring every variable exponent to be a whole number: zero, one, two, three, and so on! Expressions containing division by a variable like four over x, or fractional powers like root x, are NOT polynomials! What determines a polynomial's DEGREE? Simply locate the HIGHEST power of the variable whose coefficient is non-zero! Look at our four benchmark classes! First, CONSTANT POLYNOMIALS have degree zero, such as eight written as eight x to the power zero—just a non-zero number! Second, LINEAR POLYNOMIALS—the hero of this chapter—have degree ONE, such as three z plus seven, where the highest exponent is z to the power one! Third, QUADRATIC POLYNOMIALS have degree TWO, like x squared plus five x plus one! Fourth, CUBIC POLYNOMIALS have degree THREE, like five y cubed plus y squared plus two y minus one! Identifying the exact polynomial degree immediately predicts both the algebraic structure and the geometric shape of an expression's graph cleanly across the entire Cartesian coordinate plane!
