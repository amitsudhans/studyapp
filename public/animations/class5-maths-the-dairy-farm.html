Commutative Property • Zero & Identity Rules

Multiplication Order

🔄 Commutative Rule

a × b = b × a

Alternative Strategies • Student-Devised Methods

Mental Strategies

Slide 4 of 20

Compare the four brilliant student strategies to calculate 18 × 5 mentally:

Method 1: Halving & Doubling

• Halve the even factor: 18 ÷ 2 = 9

• Double the other factor: 5 × 2 = 10

• 9 × 10 = 90

Method 2: Partitioning (Distributive)

• Split 18 into tens and ones: (10 + 8)

• Multiply each part: (10 × 5) + (8 × 5)

• 50 + 40 = 90

Method 3: Compensation

• Round 18 to friendly 20: (20 × 5) = 100

• Subtract 2 extra groups of 5: 2 × 5 = 10

• 100 - 10 = 90

Method 4: Multiply by 10 & Halve

• Since 5 = 10 ÷ 2, multiply by 10 first: 18 × 10 = 180

• Take half of the result: 180 ÷ 2

• 180 ÷ 2 = 90

Rule of Flexibility: Choose the strategy that best exploits the special structure of the numbers involved!

Area Models • Simplifying Multiplications with 5, 25, and 50

Doubling and Halving

Slide 5 of 20

Halving one factor while doubling the other leaves the area (product) completely invariant: (a ÷ 2) × (b × 2) = a × b.

Multiplying by 5

• 48 × 5

Multiplying by 25

• 36 × 25

Multiplying by 50

Halve even factor, double 50 to 100:

• 46 × 50

• = (46 ÷ 2) × (50 × 2)

Compensation Strategy • Adjusting Over-Counting & Under-Counting

Nearest Multiple

Slide 6 of 20

The compensation strategy rounds a factor to the nearest friendly ten, multiplies, and compensates for over- or under-counting.

🔻 Over-Counting Adjustment (Subtract)

When a factor ends in 8 or 9, round UP to the nearest ten and subtract the excess:

4 × 19: 4 × (20 - 1) = (4 × 20) - (4 × 1) = 80 - 4 = 76

7 × 39: 7 × (40 - 1) = (7 × 40) - (7 × 1) = 280 - 7 = 273

6 × 48: 6 × (50 - 2) = (6 × 50) - (6 × 2) = 300 - 12 = 288

🔺 Under-Counting Adjustment (Add)

When a factor ends in 1 or 2, round DOWN to the nearest ten and add the remainder:

5 × 42: 5 × (40 + 2) = (5 × 40) + (5 × 2) = 200 + 10 = 210

8 × 31: 8 × (30 + 1) = (8 × 30) + (8 × 1) = 240 + 8 = 248

9 × 62: 9 × (60 + 2) = (9 × 60) + (9 × 2) = 540 + 18 = 558

General Algebraic Form: k × (N ± d) = (k × N) ± (k × d) (The Distributive Law over Addition and Subtraction).
