Section 2.1 • Introduction

Degrees of Polynomials & Types

If p(x) is a polynomial in x, the highest power of x in p(x) is called the degree of the polynomial.

📐
Class 10 Foundation
Linear, Quadratic & Cubic
Degree 1 ax + b (a ≠ 0)

Linear Polynomial

The highest power of the variable is 1.

• 2x - 3
• √3 x + 5
• y + √2
Degree 2 ax² + bx + c (a ≠ 0)

Quadratic Polynomial

From quadrate meaning 'square'. Highest power is 2.

• 2x² + 3x - 2/5
• y² - 2
• 4z² + 1/7
Degree 3 ax³ + bx² + cx + d

Cubic Polynomial

Highest power is 3.

• 2 - x³
• 3x³ - 2x² + x - 1
• √2 x³
⚠️

Crucial Distinction from NCERT Page 1

Expressions like 1 / (x - 1), √x + 2, and 1 / (x² + 2x + 3) are NOT polynomials because the variable has negative or fractional powers!

Section 2.2 • Geometrical Meaning

The Geometrical Meaning of Zeroes

The zeroes of a polynomial p(x) are precisely the x-coordinates of the points where the graph of y = p(x) intersects the x-axis!

y = x² - 3x - 4 Zeroes: x = -1, 4
Notice the curve cuts the x-axis exactly at (-1, 0) and (4, 0).

NCERT Pages 5-6 • Cases (i), (ii), (iii)

The 3 Geometric Cases for Quadratic Polynomials

A quadratic polynomial can have either 2 distinct zeroes, 1 zero (2 equal zeroes), or no zero. Therefore, a polynomial of degree 2 has at most two zeroes.

Case (i)

2 Distinct Zeroes

The graph cuts the x-axis at two distinct points A and A'.

Number of Zeroes = 2
Case (ii)

1 Zero (Touches x-axis)

The graph cuts the x-axis at exactly one point (two coincident points A and A' coincide).

Number of Zeroes = 1 (Equal)
Case (iii)

No Zero

The graph is completely above or completely below the x-axis, never intersecting it.

Number of Zeroes = 0
💡 Summary Rule: A polynomial of degree 2 can have at most 2 zeroes.
NCERT Pages 6-8 • Cubics & Degree n

Cubic Graphs & Degree n Theorem

In general, given a polynomial p(x) of degree n, the graph of y = p(x) intersects the x-axis at at most n points. Therefore, a polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes.

Fig. 2.6 • 3 Zeroes

y = x³ - 4x

Cuts at x = -2, 0, and 2.

3 Zeroes: {-2, 0, 2}
Fig. 2.8 • 2 Zeroes

y = x³ - x² = x²(x - 1)

Touches at x = 0 and cuts at x = 1.

2 Zeroes: {0, 1}
Fig. 2.7 • 1 Zero

y = x³

Cuts x-axis only at the origin (0, 0).

1 Zero: {0}
Universal Degree Theorem

Linear: at most 1 zero • Quadratic: at most 2 zeroes • Cubic: at most 3 zeroes • Degree n: at most n zeroes.

Section 2.3 • Core Algebraic Relation

Zeroes & Coefficients of a Quadratic

If α and β are the zeroes of the quadratic polynomial p(x) = ax² + bx + c, then (x - α) and (x - β) are factors.

Formula 1
α + β = -b / a
Sum of Zeroes = - (Coefficient of x) / (Coefficient of x²)
Formula 2
αβ = c / a
Product of Zeroes = (Constant term) / (Coefficient of x²)

Forming a Quadratic Polynomial

When the sum of zeroes S = (α + β) and the product of zeroes P = (αβ) are known, the quadratic polynomial is given by:

p(x) = k [ x² - (α + β)x + αβ ]
where k is any real non-zero constant.

NCERT Pages 21-22 • Cubic Zero-Coefficient Relations

Zeroes & Coefficients for Cubic Polynomials

If α, β, γ are the zeroes of the cubic polynomial p(x) = ax³ + bx² + cx + d (a ≠ 0), then:

Sum of Zeroes
α + β + γ = -b / a
- (Coefficient of x²) / (Coefficient of x³)
Sum of Products (Two at a time)
αβ + βγ + γα = c / a
(Coefficient of x) / (Coefficient of x³)
Product of Zeroes
αβγ = -d / a
- (Constant term) / (Coefficient of x³)
✨ NCERT Example 5 Verification: p(x) = 3x³ - 5x² - 11x - 3

Zeroes are α = 3, β = -1, γ = -1/3:

1. Sum α + β + γ:
3 + (-1) + (-1/3) = 5/3
= -(-5) / 3 = -b / a ✓
2. Sum of Products:
3(-1) + (-1)(-1/3) + (-1/3)(3) = -11/3
= -11 / 3 = c / a ✓
3. Product αβγ:
3 × (-1) × (-1/3) = 1 = 3/3
= -(-3) / 3 = -d / a ✓
