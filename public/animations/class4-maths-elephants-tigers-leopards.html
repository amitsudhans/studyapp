Winning Position Analysis

Backward Induction & Modulo 3 Invariants

📖 Spoken Classroom Lecture

Authentic Indian Male Narration

To consistently master the Nim addition game, mathematicians utilize backward reasoning to identify winning and losing positions. If our ultimate goal is to say ten, what number must we reach on the turn before? The answer is seven! From seven, if your opponent adds one to reach eight, you add two to claim ten; if your opponent adds two to reach nine, you add one to claim ten. Therefore, reaching seven guarantees victory! Now we ask: what prior milestone guarantees reaching seven? By subtracting three, we discover that controlling four ensures victory! From four, if the opponent adds one to make five, you add two to reach seven; if they add two to make six, you add one to reach seven. Working backward one step further, subtracting three from four yields one. If the first player begins by calling one, and subsequently claims four, seven, and ten, they can never be defeated! The sequence one, four, seven, and ten represents the invariant winning positions, differing by three on every step. Backward induction teaches us to begin with the desired outcome and trace logical steps backward, a profound problem-solving principle applied across computer science, economics, logistics, and competitive mathematical games.

Reverse And Add

Digit Palindromes & Multiples of Eleven

📖 Spoken Classroom Lecture

Authentic Indian Male Narration

A fascinating arithmetic pattern emerges when we take two-digit numbers, reverse their digits, and add the two values together. Suppose we select the number twenty-seven. Reversing its digits yields seventy-two. Adding twenty-seven and seventy-two produces ninety-nine! What if we select thirty-four? Its reverse is forty-three, and thirty-four plus forty-three equals seventy-seven! Notice something extraordinary about all these sums: ninety-nine, seventy-seven, fifty-five, and eighty-eight are all multiples of eleven! Why does this always occur? Let us examine the algebra of place value. Any two-digit number consisting of tens digit A and ones digit B equals ten A plus B. Its reverse equals ten B plus A. Adding them produces eleven A plus eleven B, which factors into eleven times the sum of A and B! Therefore, reversing and adding any two-digit number always generates an exact multiple of eleven. If the sum of the digits exceeds nine, the result produces a three-digit multiple: for instance, eighty-four plus forty-eight equals one hundred thirty-two, which equals eleven times twelve! Reverse-and-add patterns demystify place value mechanics, reveal beautiful algebraic palindromes, and show how underlying decimal structures produce elegant invariants, fostering deep mathematical curiosity, confidence, and deep mathematical joy, and lifelong numerical appreciation.

Neighbouring Square Grids

Grid Parity Puzzles & Combinatorial Logic

📖 Spoken Classroom Lecture

Authentic Indian Male Narration

We conclude our chapter with an ingenious combinatorial logic puzzle known as the neighbouring square grid! In a three-by-three grid containing nine square cells, a mathematician must place the digits one through nine, using each digit exactly once, such that the difference between any two adjacent, connected squares is always an odd number. Let us analyze the parity rules governing differences! The difference between two numbers is odd if and only if one number is even and the other number is odd: an even minus an odd, or an odd minus an even, always produces an odd difference. Conversely, the difference between two even numbers, or between two odd numbers, is always an even number! Therefore, for every adjacent pair of squares to have an odd difference, every even square must be bordered exclusively by odd squares, and every odd square must be bordered exclusively by even squares! This produces an alternating chessboard pattern. Counting our available digits, from one to nine there are five odd numbers (one, three, five, seven, nine) and four even numbers (two, four, six, eight). Placing the five odd digits on the corners and center, and the four even digits on the edges, solves the puzzle flawlessly!
