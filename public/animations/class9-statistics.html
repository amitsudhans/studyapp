Welcome to Chapter Fourteen: Math of Space, Surface Area and Volume! Look around your room—books, brick walls, shoeboxes, and cupboards are all CUBOIDS inside Section Fourteen Point One! A cuboid is a rectangular 3D solid bounded by six rectangular faces meeting at right angles, defined by three dimensions: length l, width w, and height h! How much wrapping paper covers the entire outside? Notice the six faces come in three identical opposite pairs! The top and bottom each have area w times l! The front and back each have area h times l! The left and right sides each have area h times w! Adding all six faces gives Total Surface Area TSA equals two times (w l plus h l plus h w)! Now how much space fits inside? Imagine stacking thin rectangular cards of area l times w, one on top of another, up to height h! Each card contributes a thin slice of area l w, so stacking up to height h sweeps out Volume V equals length times width times height—l w h! Whether measuring a wooden crate or a swimming pool, surface area measures the outer skin in square units, while volume measures the inner capacity in cubic units!

Welcome to Section Fourteen Point Two: THE RIGHT CIRCULAR CYLINDER! Tin cans, water pipes, pillars, and roller drums share this smooth circular shape! Carefully distinguish a RIGHT circular cylinder from an OBLIQUE circular cylinder! In a right circular cylinder, the central axis connecting the centres of the top and bottom circular bases stands strictly PERPENDICULAR (at ninety degrees) to both bases! In an oblique cylinder—like a leaning stack of coins—the axis tilts at a slanted angle! Now slice a right circular cylinder with an imaginary plane to discover its 3D CROSS-SECTIONS! In MEDICAL MRI SCANS! An MRI machine does not cut the human body open; instead, magnetic fields capture hundreds of thin two-dimensional cross-sectional slices along the body's axis, and computers stack those slices back into a complete three-dimensional image for doctors to inspect soft tissues, internal organs, and bones non-invasively with pinpoint medical precision every day!

Now step into Section Fourteen Point Three: RIGHT CIRCULAR CONES! Ice-cream cones, traffic pylons, birthday caps, and volcanic peaks are cones! How is a right circular cone generated geometrically? Take a right-angled triangle with vertical leg h and horizontal leg r, and spin it rapidly around its vertical leg h! The spinning horizontal leg sweeps out a flat circular base of radius r, while the spinning hypotenuse sweeps out the sloping conical wall! Every right circular cone has three key lengths meeting at a hidden right-angled triangle inside: the base radius r, the vertical perpendicular height h from the centre of the base to the pointed apex, and the SLANT HEIGHT l measured along the outer sloped edge from the rim to the apex! Whenever a problem gives you radius r and height h—say r equals three and h equals four—you immediately compute slant height l equals square root of nine plus sixteen, which is five—forming a classic three-four-five right triangle hidden neatly inside the cone!

Pyramid Volume Formula

Section 14.4 Pyramidal Shapes: V = ⅓ × Base Area × Height & Cones as Circular Pyramids

Slide 8 of 20

Discover one of the most unifying laws in all of 3D geometry inside Section Fourteen Point Four: THE UNIVERSAL PYRAMID VOLUME FORMULA! Take ANY pyramid—whether its base is a triangle, a square, a pentagon, or an irregular polygon—with base area B and perpendicular vertical height h! Its volume is ALWAYS one-third times Base Area times height: V equals one-third B h! Why does the exact same one-third factor appear for every pyramid? Think of a cube of side a sliced from its centre to all six square faces: that splits the cube into six identical square pyramids, each having base area a-squared and height a over two! Each pyramid therefore holds one-sixth of a-cubed, which equals one-third times (a-squared) times (a over two)—proving V equals one-third B h! Now watch the grand unification! Imagine increasing the number of sides of a regular polygon base from four to eight, to sixty, to infinity: the polygon approaches a smooth CIRCLE of area pi r-squared! Thus a CONE IS SIMPLY A PYRAMID WITH A CIRCULAR BASE, and one-third B h immediately becomes one-third pi r-squared h—uniting all pyramids and circular cones together under one single timeless volume law!

Slide 9 of 20

Welcome to Section Fourteen Point Five: SPHERES AND HEMISPHERES! A sphere is the set of all points in three-dimensional space at a fixed distance—radius r—from a central point O! Think of a cricket ball, soap bubble, or planet Earth! How did Archimedes prove the surface area of a sphere around two hundred twenty-five BCE? He wrapped a sphere of radius r tightly inside a circumscribed cylinder of radius r and height h equals two r! Remarkably, projecting any horizontal band of the sphere outward onto the cylinder wall preserves area EXACTLY! Therefore the sphere's surface area equals the cylinder's curved surface area: two pi r times (two r), which is FOUR pi r-squared! Cartographers use this exact cylindrical projection to make EQUIRECTANGULAR WORLD MAPS—flattening Earth onto a rectangle! However, because polar latitude circles are stretched horizontally to match the equator, regions near the poles like Greenland look wildly oversized! Finally, Archimedes proved the sphere's volume is two-thirds the cylinder's volume: two-thirds of pi r-squared times (two r), giving V equals FOUR-THIRDS pi r-cubed—a monumental geometric discovery that Archimedes valued so deeply that he asked for a sphere inside a cylinder to be carved on his tombstone!
