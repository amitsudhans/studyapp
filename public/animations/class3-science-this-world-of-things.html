See Through Glasses

Classifying Materials as Transparent, Translucent, or Opaque

Slide 4 of 20
👨‍🏫
Classroom Teacher Spoken Lecture Pure Indian Male Teacher Sound

Now let us give exact scientific names to the three marvelous light behaviors we observed during our flashlight tests! Whenever a clean material lets light pass cleanly straight through so our eyes distinguish sharp details right on the other side, scientists call it Transparent! Think about sparkling transparent examples surrounding us daily: clear glass windowpanes, empty spectacles lenses, clean glass aquariums, crisp pure water inside tumblers, and invisible classroom air! Second, whenever an object allows ONLY partial scattered light through—letting you see fuzzy silhouettes without crisp sharp reading—scientists classify it as Translucent! Memorable translucent companions include oiled butter paper, thin tissue paper lampshades, frosted bathroom windows, foggy mist, and thin milky wax! Third, whenever solid matter stops ALL light rays completely so nothing behind can ever be seen, scientists term it Opaque! Solid brick walls, sturdy wooden cupboards, metal steel dinner plates, ceramic coffee mugs, thick notebooks, and heavy stone boulders are strictly opaque! Choosing wisely among transparent, translucent, and opaque materials thoughtfully helps clever architects and house builders daily design sunny bright clear glass windows, cozy soft glowing bedroom paper lampshades, sparkling protective fish aquariums, and completely private solid wooden bathroom doors everywhere safely!

Welcome to the critical engineering divide between Rigid materials and Flexible materials!
