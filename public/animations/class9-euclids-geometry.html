Suppose rolling a fair die creates sample space S equals one, two, three, four, five, six, and someone asks specifically about rolling a number STRICTLY GREATER THAN FOUR! Only outcomes five and six satisfy that criterion! Notice five-and-six forms a neat smaller collection inside S—a SUBSET! Formally, ANY subset E contained inside sample space S is called an EVENT! Calculating equally likely probability simply divides subset sizes: P of E equals n of E DIVIDED BY n of S! Spot THREE rock-star special subsets immediately! Subset One—the EMPTY SET (zero elements inside S, such as rolling seven on a standard six-sided die) represents an IMPOSSIBLE EVENT with probability zero! Subset Three—THE COMPLEMENTARY EVENT NOT-E gathers every elementary outcome inside S lying strictly OUTSIDE subset E! Adding both probabilities always equals one, unlocking the golden shortcut: P OF NOT-E EQUALS ONE MINUS P OF E—ideal for solving tricky 'at least one' probability calculations effortlessly!
