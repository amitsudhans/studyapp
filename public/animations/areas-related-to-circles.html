Section 11.1 • Geometric Anatomy

Sectors vs. Segments: Slices vs. Chords

Every circle can be subdivided into two fundamental regions: Sectors (enclosed by two radii and an arc) and Segments (enclosed between a chord and an arc).

🎯
Default Convention
Sector = Minor Sector unless stated

1. The Sector of a Circle (Fig. 11.1)

Pizza Slice

Enclosed by two radii OA, OB and the corresponding arc APB. Central angle is ∠AOB = θ. Major sector angle is 360° - θ.

2. The Segment of a Circle (Fig. 11.2)

Chord Cap

Enclosed between a chord AB and the corresponding arc APB. Unshaded region is the major segment AQB!

💡 Fundamental Relationship: Area of Segment = Area of Sector - Area of Triangle OAB!
Formulas & Derivations • Unitary Method

Derivation of Sector Area & Arc Length

By applying the simple Unitary Method to the complete 360° circle, we derive the exact expressions for both sector area and perimeter arc length.

1. Area of a Sector (Angle θ)

When centre angle is 360° ➔ Area = πr²
When centre angle is 1° ➔ Area = πr² / 360
When centre angle is θ:
Area = (θ / 360°) × πr²
Major Sector Area = πr² - Minor Sector Area or [(360° - θ)/360°] × πr²

2. Length of an Arc (Angle θ)

When centre angle is 360° ➔ Length = 2πr
When centre angle is 1° ➔ Length = 2πr / 360
When centre angle is θ:
Arc Length l = (θ / 360°) × 2πr
Useful Shortcut: Area of sector can also be expressed as Area = ½ × l × r!
Exercise 11.1 Q14 MCQ Check: Sector Area of angle p and radius R = (p / 720°) × 2πR²

Example 2 • High-Scoring Trigonometric Geometry

The 120° Segment: Solving Obtuse Central Angles

When the central angle is obtuse (120°), we split the isosceles triangle OAB with perpendicular bisector OM into two 30°-60°-90° right triangles!

Fig. 11.7 Altitude Splitting (r = 21 cm, θ = 120°)

1. Sector Area OAYB:
(120° / 360°) × (22 / 7) × 21 × 21 = 462 cm²
2. Altitude & Base in Δ OMA:
OM = 21 × cos 60° = 21 × ½ = 21 / 2 cm
AM = 21 × sin 60° = 21√3 / 2 cm ➔ AB = 21√3 cm
3. Area of Triangle Δ OAB:
½ × AB × OM = ½ × 21√3 × (21 / 2) = 441√3 / 4 cm²
4. Area of Segment AYB:
462 - (441√3 / 4) = 21/4 (88 - 21√3) cm²
Area of Segment = 462 - 190.95 = 271.05 cm²
Exercise 11.1 Q3 • Real-World Time Modeling

Clock Hands: Area Swept in 5 Minutes

"The length of the minute hand of a clock is 14 cm. Find the area swept by the minute hand in 5 minutes."

Clock Face Geometry (14 cm Minute Hand)

Step-by-Step Angle & Area Formulation:
1. Angle in 5 Minutes:
In 60 minutes = 360°
In 1 minute = 360° / 60 = 6°
In 5 minutes = 5 × 6° = 30°
2. Area Swept (Sector of 30°):
Area = (30° / 360°) × (22 / 7) × 14 × 14
= (1 / 12) × 22 × 2 × 14
= (1 / 12) × 616 = 154 / 3 cm²
Area Swept = 154/3 cm² ≈ 51.33 cm²
Exercise 11.1 Q8 • Quadrant Grazing

Horse in a Square Field: Area & Rope Extension

A horse is pegged at one corner of a 15m square field. The grazing boundary forms a quadrant (90° sector)!

Fig. 11.8 Quadrant Sectors (90°)

(i) Area with 5 m rope:
A₁ = ¼ × 3.14 × 5² = ¼ × 78.5 = 19.625 m²
(ii) Area with 10 m rope:
A₂ = ¼ × 3.14 × 10² = ¼ × 314 = 78.5 m²
Increase in Grazing Area:
ΔA = 78.5 - 19.625 = 58.875 m²
Net Grazing Area Increase = 58.875 m²
Exercise 11.1 Q9 • Symmetry & Wire Length

The Silver Brooch: Perimeter Wire & 10 Sectors

"A brooch is made with silver wire in the form of a circle with diameter 35 mm. The wire is also used in making 5 diameters dividing into 10 equal sectors."

Fig. 11.9 10-Sector Symmetry

(i) Total Silver Wire Required:
Circumference = πd = (22 / 7) × 35 = 110 mm
5 Diameters = 5 × 35 = 175 mm
Total Wire = 110 + 175 = 285 mm
(ii) Area of Each of the 10 Sectors:
Radius r = 35 / 2 mm
Each Sector = (1 / 10) × πr²
= (1 / 10) × (22 / 7) × (35 / 2) × (35 / 2)
Area = 385 / 4 mm² = 96.25 mm²
Wire = 285 mm | Sector Area = 96.25 mm²
Exercise 11.1 Q13 • Hexagonal Design Cost

Round Table Cover: Cost of 6 Equal Segment Designs

"A round table cover has 6 equal designs. Radius = 28 cm. Find cost at ₹0.35 per cm² using √3 = 1.7."

Fig. 11.11 6-Segment Hexagon (θ = 60°)

Each Sector Angle = 360° / 6 = 60°
Sector Area = (60° / 360°) × (22 / 7) × 28 × 28 = 410.67 cm²
Equilateral Δ Area = (√3 / 4) × 28² = (1.7 / 4) × 784 = 333.2 cm²
One Design Segment = 410.67 - 333.2 = 77.47 cm²
Total 6 Designs = 6 × 77.47 = 464.8 cm²
Cost = 464.8 × ₹0.35 = ₹162.68
Total Cost of 6 Designs = ₹162.68
