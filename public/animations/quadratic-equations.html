Now lock in the official mathematical definition from Section Four Point Two! ANY equation expressible in the standard descending-power form a x squared plus b x plus c equals zero, where a, b, and c are real numbers and leading coefficient a is STRICTLY NONZERO (a not equal to zero), is called a QUADRATIC EQUATION in variable x! Zoom sharply inside Sridharacharya's Quadratic Formula onto that crucial algebraic expression locked beneath the square-root roof: b squared minus 4 a c! Because this single number DISCRIMINATES completely between distinct real roots, equal coincident roots, and impossible non-real roots, mathematicians honor it with the capital symbol D—the DISCRIMINANT: D equals b squared minus 4 a c! Think logically about WHY real-number arithmetic reacts so dramatically to the sign of D inside square root of D! Case A: Whenever D is STRICTLY POSITIVE (D greater than zero), square root of D produces a clear positive real value, so adding root D gives one root while subtracting root D gives a SECOND distinct root! Case B: Whenever D equals EXACTLY ZERO (D equals zero), square root of zero collapses to zero, forcing plus 0 and minus 0 to return the IDENTICAL fraction minus b over 2a twice—two EQUAL real roots! Case C: Whenever D turns NEGATIVE (D less than zero), no real number squared ever gives negative D, so square root of D holds ZERO real values—meaning ZERO REAL ROOTS exist anywhere along the continuous real number line! Thus checking D first predicts instantly what kind of roots exist!
