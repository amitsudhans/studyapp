Build clear 3D vocabulary cleanly inside Section Four Point Two! Any closed three-dimensional solid bounded entirely by flat polygonal surfaces is called a POLYHEDRON! Count its three structural parts carefully: flat polygonal surfaces are FACES (F); straight line segments where two faces meet are EDGES (E); and sharp corner points where edges meet are VERTICES (V)! Verify a familiar brick-shaped cuboid right away: 6 rectangular faces, 12 straight edges, and 8 sharp corner vertices! Distinguish two major polyhedron families! Family One: PRISMS! Unlike prisms, a solid pyramid stands firmly upon ONE single flat polygonal base, while flat triangular side faces rise slantwise upward from every base edge and converge together neatly at ONE single sharp top corner point called the APEX! A triangular pyramid—whose 4 bounding flat surfaces are all flat triangles—carries the special classical geometric name TETRAHEDRON (counting exactly 4 faces, 6 straight edges, and 4 sharp corner vertices)!
