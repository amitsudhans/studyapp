Probability Axioms Bounds

Section 14.1 Rules: 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1, Sure Event (1), Impossible Event (0) & Sum of Elementary Events = 1

Slide 3 of 20
👨‍🏫
Classroom Teacher Spoken Lecture Pure Indian Male Teacher Sound

Lock in FOUR unbreakable laws governing every single probability number in the universe inside Section Fourteen Point One! Law One, Universal Bounds: Since favourable counts ALWAYS sit cleanly between zero and total possible outcomes, EVERY valid probability P(E) satisfies ZERO less than or equal to P(E) less than or equal to ONE! That means negative numbers like minus 1.5, fractions bigger than one like five over four, and percentages above 100 percent like 105 percent can NEVER represent any probability! Law Two, Impossible Event: An event completely impossible to occur has ZERO favourable outcomes, so P(E) equals ZERO! Example (Ex 14.1 Q6): picking an orange-flavored candy blindly from a bag containing ONLY lemon candies has probability ZERO! Law Three, Sure or Certain Event: An event guaranteed to happen EVERY single trial equals ONE! Picking a lemon candy from that lemon-only bag has probability ONE! Law Four, Elementary Events: An event having only a SINGLE simple outcome is called an elementary event, and adding together the probabilities of ALL possible elementary events of any random experiment (such as drawing red, blue, or yellow balls inside Textbook Example Two) ALWAYS equals EXACTLY ONE every single time cleanly!

Meet the fastest subtraction shortcut in all of Probability inside Section Fourteen Point One: Complementary Events! For any event E, the exact opposite event 'not E' (written E-bar, Ē) consists of EVERY outcome inside the sample space where E does NOT happen! Since on every trial either E happens or E does not happen with zero overlap, together they cover one hundred percent of all possible outcomes: P(E) plus P(not E) ALWAYS equals EXACTLY ONE! Rearranging gives the golden Complementary Formula: P(not E) equals ONE MINUS P(E)! Test Exercise Fourteen Point One Question Five instantly! If P(E) equals 0.05, what is P(not E)? Align your decimal places carefully from 1.00: 1.00 minus 0.05 equals exact ZERO POINT NINE FIVE (careful—never mistakenly write 0.5 or 0.995)! Now inspect Question Seven, the classic Same Birthday problem! In a small group of just two students, the probability they do NOT share the exact same birthday is 0.992! What is the probability BOTH share the EXACT same birthday? Apply the Complement Law immediately: 1.000 minus 0.992 equals exact 0.008! Likewise in Example Four tennis match, winning match probability 0.62 leaves losing match probability exact 0.38 cleanly every single time!

Never fall for the classic Two-Coin 1/3 Fallacy! Many students wrongly claim: tossing two coins gives 'two heads', 'two tails', or 'one of each', so each probability must be 1/3! WRONG! Why? Two distinct unbiased coins tossed simultaneously produce FOUR equally likely ordered outcomes: {HH, HT, TH, TT}! Check Example Seven right away: Probability of AT LEAST ONE HEAD covers {HH, HT, TH} = 3 over 4! Probability of AT MOST ONE HEAD covers {HT, TH, TT} = 3 over 4! One, Theoretical Probability Formula: Assuming all outcomes are equally likely, P(E) equals Number of Outcomes Favourable to E divided by Number of All Possible Outcomes! Two, Probability Bounds & Extremes: Every probability stays strictly inside 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1! Impossible events ALWAYS have probability ZERO, while Sure or Certain events ALWAYS have probability ONE!
