What are Life Processes? Molecular Movement & The Virus Controversy (Section 5.1)

"The processes which together perform this maintenance job in living organisms—preventing breakdown and maintaining order—are life processes."

How do we distinguish what is alive from what is inanimate? While visible movements like running, chewing, or breathing mark active animals, plants grow silently without obvious locomotion. True life operates at the microscopic molecular scale, constantly repairing cellular architecture against environmental decay.

Dr. Sophia • Biology Teacher
Teacher's Live Audio Commentary Web Speech Narration

"Welcome students to Chapter 5: Life Processes! How do we know something is alive? It isn't just about running or shouting. True life is defined by constant, invisible molecular movements repairing cells from within! Even viruses remain inert crystals until they enter a host cell. Together, nutrition, respiration, transportation, and excretion keep living organisms organized and alive!"

Visible Movement vs Invisible Molecular Motion

Visible locomotion is insufficient as the sole definition of life. Plants move via slow phototropism or growth, and sleeping animals display minimal exterior motion. Biologists confirm that invisible molecular movements—the continuous shuttling of ions, proteins, and metabolites across cell membranes—are absolute prerequisites for maintaining cellular order against entropy.

The Virus Controversy & The 4 Master Life Processes

• Viruses: Lack any independent molecular movement or metabolic machinery until they invade and hijack a host cell's genetic apparatus. This absence of intrinsic molecular movement fuels the enduring debate over whether viruses are truly living or non-living.
• The Four Core Life Processes: To maintain organized structures, all living organisms depend on: (1) Nutrition (acquiring external energy sources), (2) Respiration (cellular oxidation of food to yield ATP), (3) Transportation (internal distribution of fluids and materials), and (4) Excretion (removal of toxic metabolic wastes).

Section 5.2.1 • Autotrophs Slide 2 of 21

Autotrophic Nutrition & The Master Photosynthesis Equation (Section 5.2.1)

"Autotrophs convert simple inorganic carbon dioxide and water into complex stored carbohydrates in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll."

Autotrophs are Earth's primary solar energy converters. Green plants and photosynthetic bacteria capture photons to synthesize glucose, storing surplus energy as starch in plants, just as humans store glucose reserves as glycogen in our liver and muscles.

Dr. Sophia • Biology Teacher
Teacher's Live Audio Commentary Web Speech Narration

"Look at the master chemical equation that powers our planet: carbon dioxide and water combine under sunlight and chlorophyll to forge glucose and release oxygen! Plants store excess food as starch, while our bodies store reserves as glycogen. Desert plants have a clever adaptation: they capture carbon dioxide at night to conserve water, and finish photosynthesis during the day!"

The Balanced Equation of Photosynthesis

$$6\text{CO}_2 + 12\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow[\text{Sunlight}]{\text{Chlorophyll}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
• Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$): Absorbed from atmospheric air via leaf stomata.
• Water ($H_2O$): Absorbed from soil via root xylem.
• Energy Reserve: Synthesized glucose powers metabolic work; unconsumed carbohydrates are polymerised into starch (in plants) or glycogen (in animals).

The 3 Sequential Photochemical Events

1. Absorption: Light energy is trapped by chlorophyll pigments in thylakoids.
2. Photolysis & Energy Transduction: Light energy converts to chemical energy; water molecules split into hydrogen and oxygen: $2H_2O \to 4H^+ + 4e^- + O_2\uparrow$.
3. Reduction: Carbon dioxide is enzymatically reduced to carbohydrates ($C_6H_{12}O_6$).
Desert Plant Adaptation: In arid habitats, cacti take up $CO_2$ at night through open stomata, forming an organic acid intermediate, and reduce it using sunlight trapped during the day!

The Photosynthesis Solar Energy Converter

Chlorophyll absorbs sunlight; water splits and CO₂ reduces to starch.

Activity 5.2 • Gaseous Exchange Slide 4 of 21

Stomatal Guard Cells & The Potassium Hydroxide Carbon Dioxide Experiment (Activity 5.2 & Fig 5.3)

"The opening and closing of the stomatal pore is a function of guard cells: swelling when water flows in, shrinking when water is lost. Potassium hydroxide absorbs CO2!"

How do plants inhale carbon dioxide without dehydrating to death? Specialized kidney-shaped guard cells act as hydraulic biological valves, regulating stomatal apertures in response to turgor pressure.

Dr. Sophia • Biology Teacher
Teacher's Live Audio Commentary Web Speech Narration

"Notice the stomatal guard cells! When water floods into them, they swell like curved balloons, pulling the pore wide open for gas exchange. When water leaves, they shrink and close the pore to stop dehydration! In Activity 5.2, potassium hydroxide in the bell jar absorbs all carbon dioxide, starving the plant so it cannot produce any starch!"

Hydraulic Stomatal Regulation (Fig 5.3)

• Stomata: Microscopic pores predominantly on the underside of leaves for gas exchange ($CO_2$ in, $O_2$ out).
• Guard Cell Mechanics: Flank each pore with differentially thickened cell walls (thick inner wall, elastic outer wall). When water flows into guard cells, they become turgid and swell, bowing outward to pull the pore open. When water exits, guard cells become flaccid and shrink, snapping the pore closed to prevent transpiration loss.

Activity 5.2: Proving Carbon Dioxide is Essential

• Two healthy potted plants (A and B) destarched for 3 days in the dark.
• Plant A is enclosed under a glass bell jar alongside a watch-glass containing Potassium Hydroxide ($KOH$) pellets, which avidly chemically absorb $CO_2$: $2KOH + CO_2 \to K_2CO_3 + H_2O$.
• Plant B is enclosed under a bell jar without $KOH$. Both jars are sealed airtight with vaseline and placed in sunlight for 2 hours.
• Result: Leaves of Plant B test positive for starch (blue-black with iodine); leaves of Plant A test negative, proving $CO_2$ is essential for photosynthesis.

Heterotrophic Nutrition Strategies: Saprophytes, Holozoic & Parasites (Section 5.2.2)

"Heterotrophs survival depends directly or indirectly on autotrophs. Different strategies have evolved: external breakdown, internal holozoic digestion, or parasitic exploitation."

Unable to harness solar photons, heterotrophs have evolved extraordinary ecological adaptations to harvest energy. From fungi digesting bread outside their bodies to predatory hunters and parasitic tapeworms, nutrition reflects body design and mobility.

Dr. Sophia • Biology Teacher
Teacher's Live Audio Commentary Web Speech Narration

"Heterotrophs obtain energy through three diverse strategies! Saprophytes like mushrooms and bread moulds break down food outside their bodies with enzymes and absorb the liquid soup. Holozoic organisms like humans and lions take whole food inside to digest internally. And parasites like Cuscuta, ticks, and tapeworms feed off living hosts without killing them!"

1. Saprophytic / Absorptive Nutrition

Organisms that secrete digestive enzymes directly onto dead, decaying organic matter, breaking down complex polymers into simple soluble nutrients outside their bodies, and then absorbing them across cell surfaces. Examples: Bread moulds (Rhizopus), yeast, and mushrooms.

2. Holozoic & 3. Parasitic Nutrition

• Holozoic Nutrition: Organisms ingest solid, complex food whole, which is subsequently digested, absorbed, assimilated, and egested internally using an alimentary canal. Examples: Humans, dogs, cattle, and Amoeba.
• Parasitic Nutrition: Organisms derive nourishment directly from the tissues or fluids of a living host plant or animal without killing it immediately. Examples: Cuscuta (amar-bel), ticks, lice, leeches, and tape-worms.

Nutrition in Single-Celled Organisms: Amoeba Pseudopodia & Paramecium Cilia (Section 5.2.3 & Fig 5.5)

Dr. Sophia • Biology Teacher
Teacher's Live Audio Commentary Web Speech Narration

"Watch the marvel of single-celled hunting! Amoeba reaches out with flowing arms called pseudopodia, fusing them together to swallow a food particle into a food vacuole where enzymes digest it. Holozoic Phagocytosis in Amoeba (Fig 5.5)

1. Ingestion: When sensing a food particle, Amoeba extends temporary finger-like projections of cytoplasm called pseudopodia ('false feet') around the food.
2. Food-Vacuole Formation: Pseudopodia fuse over the particle, enclosing it inside an intracellular membrane-bound food-vacuole.
3. Digestion & Absorption: Digestive enzymes enter the vacuole from the cytoplasm, breaking down complex polymers into simple soluble molecules that diffuse directly into the cytoplasm.
4. Egestion: The residual undigested waste migrates to the cell surface membrane, ruptures outward, and is expelled.

Ciliary Feeding in Paramecium

Unlike shapeless Amoeba, Paramecium possesses a definite, rigid pellicle shape. Food cannot enter at random points; it is captured exclusively at a specialized cytostome (oral groove / specific spot). Coordinated microscopic beating of thousands of hair-like cilia covering its cell surface generates directional water currents that sweep food particles directly into the oral groove.

Section 5.2.4 • Complete Digestion Slide 8 of 21

Stomach Digestion & Complete Breakdown in the Small Intestine: Bile, Pancreatic Juice & Villi (Section 5.2.4)

"The small intestine is the site of complete digestion of carbohydrates, proteins and fats. Villi provide massive surface area for nutrient absorption into blood."

From the acidic churning bath of the stomach where gastric glands secrete hydrochloric acid and pepsin, to the alkaline lumen of the small intestine bathed in bile and pancreatic enzymes, human digestion achieves chemical completion before millions of microscopic villi absorb nutrients.

Dr. Sophia • Biology Teacher
Teacher's Live Audio Commentary Web Speech Narration

"Inside your stomach, hydrochloric acid creates a powerful acidic bath that activates pepsin to digest proteins, while thick mucus protects the stomach lining from burns! Next, food enters the small intestine—the master site of digestion! Liver bile neutralizes the acid and breaks down fat globules, while pancreatic enzymes finish the job. The Gastric Trio: Acid, Pepsin & Mucus

Gastric glands in the muscular stomach wall secrete:
1. Hydrochloric Acid ($HCl$): Creates an acidic environment ($pH \approx 1.5-2.0$) essential to activate inactive pepsinogen into active pepsin, and sterilizes ingested food by killing microbes.
2. Pepsin: Protein-digesting enzyme that cleaves proteins into peptides.
3. Mucus: Coats the stomach wall, shielding delicate tissues from self-digestion and burning ulcers caused by concentrated $HCl$ (preventing hyperacidity).

Small Intestine: Complete Digestion & Villi Absorption

• Liver (Bile Juice): Alkaline bile neutralizes acidic chyme and emulsifies large fat globules into tiny droplets, multiplying surface area for lipase.
• Pancreas: Secretes pancreatic juice with Trypsin (digests proteins) and Lipase (digests emulsified fats).
• Intestinal Enzymes: Complete hydrolysis into final monomers: Carbohydrates $\to$ Glucose; Proteins $\to$ Amino Acids; Fats $\to$ Fatty Acids + Glycerol.
• Villi: Millions of microscopic finger-like projections lined with capillaries and lacteals, vastly increasing surface area to absorb digested nutrients into the bloodstream.

The Three Pathways of Glucose Breakdown & The ATP Energy Currency (Section 5.3, Fig 5.8)

"In all organisms, the first step is the breakdown of glucose (6C) into pyruvate (3C) in the cytoplasm. Pyruvate then takes three distinct metabolic routes."

Whether an organism thrives in oxygen or suffuses in anaerobic depths, life begins cellular respiration with identical glycolysis in the cytoplasm. But what happens next depends entirely on oxygen availability, mitochondrial machinery, and sudden athletic exertion!

Dr. Sophia • Biology Teacher
Teacher's Live Audio Commentary Web Speech Narration

"Study this crucial flowchart of life: all cells first split 6-carbon glucose into 3-carbon pyruvate in the cytoplasm! If oxygen is absent, yeast produces ethanol and carbon dioxide. In your muscle cells during a frantic sprint, oxygen runs low, forming lactic acid that triggers painful cramps! But inside mitochondria with ample oxygen, pyruvate oxidizes completely into carbon dioxide, water, and massive amounts of ATP energy!"

Step 1: Cytoplasmic Glycolysis (Universal)

Inside the cytoplasm of all living cells, a 6-carbon glucose molecule is cleaved into two 3-carbon molecules of Pyruvate with a small release of energy. Oxygen is NOT required for this initial step.

The 3 Divergent Pyruvate Fates (Fig 5.8)

1. Absence of Oxygen (Anaerobic / Yeast Fermentation): Occurs in cytoplasm $\to$ Ethanol (2C) + $CO_2$ + 2 ATP.
2. Lack of Oxygen (Human Muscle Cells during heavy sprinting): Oxygen demand outpaces supply $\to$ Pyruvate converts to Lactic acid (3C) + Energy. Accumulation of lactic acid crystals causes painful muscle cramps!
3. Presence of Oxygen (Aerobic / Mitochondria): Complete oxidation $\to$ $6CO_2 + 6H_2O$ + 36-38 ATP (immensely higher energy yield!).
• ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate): Universal energy currency ($ADP + P_i \to ATP$). Breaking terminal phosphate bond releases $30.5\text{ kJ/mol}$.

Dr. Sophia • Biology Teacher
Teacher's Live Audio Commentary Web Speech Narration

Alveolar Architecture & Breathing Mechanics

• Alveolar Network: The lungs contain roughly 300-500 million balloon-like alveoli. If flattened, their surface area exceeds $80\text{ m}^2$, maximizing gas diffusion rates.
• Inhalation (Inspiration): Diaphragm contracts and flattens; rib muscles contract lifting the ribcage upward and outward. The enlarged thoracic cavity drops internal pressure, sucking air in to inflate alveoli.
• Exhalation (Expiration): Diaphragm relaxes into its dome shape; ribs descend, pushing air out.
• Residual Volume: Lungs always retain residual air volume so gas exchange continues uninterrupted even between breaths.

Why Diffusion Fails & The Role of Haemoglobin

In large multicellular animals, simple diffusion is impossibly slow—it would take an estimated 3 years for an oxygen molecule to diffuse from your lungs to your toes! Instead, the respiratory pigment Haemoglobin in red blood cells binds oxygen with high affinity ($Hb + 4O_2 \rightleftharpoons Hb(O_2)_4$), delivering oxygen to tissues in seconds. Carbon dioxide is more water-soluble and is carried primarily dissolved as bicarbonate in blood plasma.

Section 5.4.1 • Cardiovascular Pump Slide 13 of 21

The Human Heart: 4 Chambers, Pumping Mechanics & Double Circulation (Section 5.4.1 & Figs 5.10, 5.11)

"The heart has four chambers to prevent oxygen-rich blood from mixing with carbon dioxide-rich blood, enabling double circulation for high energy demands."

Beating over 100,000 times a day, the human heart is a fist-sized double pump. Its four-chambered architecture completely separates oxygenated and deoxygenated blood streams, powering double circulation to fuel our high mammalian body temperature.

Dr. Sophia • Biology Teacher
Teacher's Live Audio Commentary Web Speech Narration

"Meet your life's tireless pump: the four-chambered heart! Oxygen-rich blood from the lungs enters the left atrium and is blasted by the muscular left ventricle into the aorta to feed the body! Deoxygenated blood returns to the right atrium and is pumped to the lungs by the right ventricle. Because blood travels through the heart twice in every complete circuit, it is called double circulation!"

4-Chambered Anatomy & The Cardiac Pumping Cycle (Fig 5.10)

1. Pulmonary Return: Oxygen-rich blood from lungs enters the relaxed Left Atrium via pulmonary veins.
2. Left Ventricle Contraction: Left atrium contracts, transferring blood to the thick muscular Left Ventricle, which pumps oxygenated blood under high pressure into the Aorta to body organs.
3. Systemic Return: Deoxygenated blood from body returns via superior and inferior Vena Cava into the Right Atrium.
4. Pulmonary Ejection: Right atrium transfers blood to Right Ventricle, which pumps it via the Pulmonary Artery to lungs for re-oxygenation.
• Valves: Atrioventricular and semilunar valves ensure strictly unidirectional flow, preventing backflow.
• Thick Ventricular Walls: Ventricles have much thicker muscular myocardium than atria because they pump blood against systemic resistance.

Evolutionary Heart Chambers & Double Circulation (Fig 5.11)

• Fishes (2 Chambers): Single circulation; blood pumped to gills for oxygenation and flows directly to body before returning once.
• Amphibians & Reptiles (3 Chambers): 2 atria and 1 ventricle; tolerate partial mixing of blood (cold-blooded / poikilothermic).
• Birds & Mammals (4 Chambers): Complete septum separation; blood passes through heart twice during one complete body circuit (Pulmonary loop + Systemic loop = Double Circulation). Provides optimal oxygen delivery to maintain warm-blooded constant body temperature ($37^\circ\text{C}$).

Section 5.4.1 • Hemodynamics Slide 14 of 21

Blood Pressure (Sphygmomanometer), Blood Vessels (Arteries vs Veins) & Platelet Clotting (Section 5.4.1)

"Normal blood pressure is 120/80 mm of Hg, measured with a sphygmomanometer. Arteries are thick and elastic; veins have valves; platelets clot to plug leaks."

Under what hydraulic pressure does blood course through your vascular network? Blood pressure surges during ventricular systole and relaxes in diastole. Arteries withstand fierce arterial pulses, veins guide blood home with non-return valves, and circulating platelets stand guard against haemorrhage.

Dr. Sophia • Biology Teacher
Teacher's Live Audio Commentary Web Speech Narration

"Listen to the pulse of your vascular network! Normal blood pressure is 120 over 80 millimetres of mercury, measured with a sphygmomanometer cuff. Arteries have thick, muscular, elastic walls to withstand high pumping pressure. Veins have thinner walls with one-way valves that prevent blood from falling backward. And if an injury occurs, platelets immediately form a blood clot to seal the leak!"

Blood Pressure & The Sphygmomanometer

• Blood Pressure (BP): Force exerted by blood against vessel walls; highest in arteries, diminishing in capillaries and veins.
• Systolic Pressure: Arterial pressure during ventricular contraction (systole) $\approx$ $120\text{ mm of Hg}$.
• Diastolic Pressure: Arterial pressure during ventricular relaxation (diastole) $\approx$ $80\text{ mm of Hg}$.
• Sphygmomanometer: Clinical instrument with inflatable cuff used to measure BP.
• Hypertension (High BP): Arteriolar constriction increases peripheral resistance, risking internal bleeding, stroke, and arterial rupture.

Arteries vs Veins vs Capillaries & Platelet Clotting

• Arteries: Carry oxygenated blood away from heart (except pulmonary artery). Thick, elastic muscular walls to withstand high surge pressures; no valves.
• Veins: Carry deoxygenated blood toward heart (except pulmonary vein). Thin walls under low pressure; possess unidirectional valves to prevent backflow.
• Capillaries: Microscopic vessels with walls only one-cell thick, facilitating direct diffusion of nutrients, gases, and wastes between blood and tissues.
• Platelet Clotting: In injury, platelets aggregate and release thromboplastin, forming a fibrin mesh clot to plug leaks, halt haemorrhage, and preserve hydraulic pressure.

Clinical Hemodynamics: Sphygmomanometer & Vessels

Sphygmomanometer measures systolic (120 mm Hg) and diastolic (80 mm Hg) pressure.

Section 5.4.2 • Fluid Mechanics Slide 15 of 21

Lymph (Tissue Fluid) & Water Transport in Plants: Xylem, Root Pressure & Transpiration Pull (Sections 5.4.1 & 5.4.2)

"Transpiration creates a suction pull that lifts water hundreds of feet up tall trees. Lymph carries digested fats and drains excess interstitial fluid."

Beyond blood, our bodies circulate lymph—a colourless tissue fluid that reclaims extracellular proteins and absorbs dietary fats. In the plant kingdom, giant redwood trees lift metric tons of water to leaf canopies through xylem vessels without a muscular pump, driven by solar evaporation and transpiration pull!

Dr. Sophia • Biology Teacher
Teacher's Live Audio Commentary Web Speech Narration

"Meet lymph: the silent white river of immunity that drains excess fluid from tissues and transports digested fats! In plants, look how giant trees lift water hundreds of feet into the sky without any mechanical heart! During the night, active root pressure pushes water upward. But during the sunny day, water evaporating from leaf stomata creates an immense suction force called transpiration pull!"

Lymph (Tissue Fluid) Functions

Through microscopic pores in capillary walls, plasma fluid, low-molecular-weight proteins, and white blood cells escape into intercellular tissue spaces, forming lymph (tissue fluid). Colourless and with fewer proteins than blood plasma, lymph enters lymphatic capillaries $\to$ lymph vessels $\to$ drains into large veins. Roles: Transports digested dietary fats from intestinal lacteals, drains surplus extracellular fluid, and houses disease-fighting lymphocytes.

Xylem Transport: Root Pressure & Transpiration Pull (Activity 5.8 & Fig 5.12)

• Root Pressure (Night): Active ion uptake by root epidermal cells creates an osmotic gradient, pulling water into root xylem and generating a steady upward hydrostatic push.
• Transpiration Pull (Day): Evaporation of water vapour from leaf stomata (Activity 5.8) generates negative hydrostatic tension in leaf mesophyll cells. Due to water's high cohesion and adhesion, this tension exerts a tremendous suction pull that draws continuous water columns up xylem vessels and tracheids to the summit of tall trees, simultaneously providing evaporative cooling.

Section 5.4.2 • Phloem Bio-energetics Slide 16 of 21

Plant Food Translocation: Phloem Sieve Tubes & ATP Pressure Flow (Section 5.4.2)

"Translocation in phloem is an active process utilizing energy from ATP to move sucrose, amino acids, and hormones in both upward and downward directions."

While xylem transport is largely passive, unidirectional, and driven by solar evaporation, the transport of soluble photosynthetic products—translocation—is an active, bidirectional, ATP-powered biological pipeline running through phloem sieve tubes and companion cells.

Dr. Sophia • Biology Teacher
Teacher's Live Audio Commentary Web Speech Narration

"Unlike xylem, phloem transport is an active, living, two-way street! Photosynthetic leaves load sweet sucrose into sieve tubes using ATP energy. This draws water in by osmosis, building high hydrostatic pressure that pushes the sugary sap to wherever the plant needs it—down to root storage in autumn, or up to blossoming buds in spring!"

Phloem Architecture & Translocation Content

• Phloem Components: Sieve tubes (living cells with perforated sieve plates) supported by metabolically active nucleated companion cells, phloem parenchyma, and phloem fibres.
• Transported Solutes: Soluble sucrose, amino acids, phytohormones, and other synthesized metabolites.
• Bidirectional Delivery: Unlike xylem (which only flows upward), phloem transports materials both upward and downward, delivering sugars from source leaves to storage sinks (roots, fruits, seeds) and growing buds.

The ATP Osmotic Pressure-Flow Mechanism

1. Active Loading: Sucrose is actively loaded from photosynthetic mesophyll cells into phloem sieve tubes using metabolic energy from ATP.
2. Osmotic Water Entry: High solute concentration lowers water potential; water rushes in from adjacent xylem via osmosis, generating elevated turgor hydrostatic pressure.
3. Bulk Flow to Sinks: High pressure in the source sieve tube forces the sugar sap toward regions of lower hydrostatic pressure (sinks, such as root storage cells or spring buds), where sucrose is unloaded and consumed.

Section 5.5 • Renal Physiology Slide 17 of 21

Human Excretion: Nephron Filtration, Selective Reabsorption & Hemodialysis (Section 5.5, Figs 5.13 & 5.14)

"Each kidney contains a million nephrons. Initial filtrate is 180 litres daily, but 99% is selectively reabsorbed, excreting just 1 to 2 litres of urine!"

Metabolism continuously produces toxic nitrogenous wastes like urea and uric acid. The human urinary system filters your entire blood volume dozens of times every day through two million microscopic nephrons, performing precision ultrafiltration and selective reabsorption.

Dr. Sophia • Biology Teacher
Teacher's Live Audio Commentary Web Speech Narration

"Inside your two kidneys work two million microscopic marvels called nephrons! High blood pressure in the glomerulus squeezes 180 litres of initial filtrate into Bowman's capsule every single day! But your body doesn't waste that fluid: 99 percent of water, glucose, and amino acids are selectively reabsorbed, leaving just one to two litres of concentrated urine. And for kidney failure patients, hemodialysis uses semipermeable tubes to save lives!"

Excretory Anatomy & Nephron Function (Figs 5.13 & 5.14)

• Urinary Organs: Pair of bean-shaped kidneys in the dorsal abdomen $\to$ muscular ureters $\to$ urinary bladder (nervous controlled storage) $\to$ urethra.
• Bowman's Capsule & Glomerulus: High-pressure capillary knot (glomerulus) filters blood plasma into Bowman's capsule (ultrafiltration).
• Selective Reabsorption: As filtrate traverses coiled tubules (PCT, Loop of Henle, DCT), essential nutrients (glucose, amino acids, vitamins, mineral salts, and water) are selectively pumped back into peritubular blood capillaries.
• Urine Volume Regulation: Governed by hydration status and circulating antidiuretic hormone (ADH). Out of $180\text{ L}$ of daily initial filtrate, over $99\%$ is reabsorbed; only $1-2\text{ L}$ of concentrated urine is excreted!

Artificial Kidney (Hemodialysis) & Plant Excretion

• Hemodialysis: In kidney failure, patient's blood is passed through semipermeable cellophane coils immersed in dialysing fluid matching blood's osmotic pressure but devoid of nitrogenous wastes. Urea and excess salts diffuse out; purified blood is warmed and re-infused. Key Difference: Hemodialysis involves NO selective tubular reabsorption.
• Plant Excretion: Plants shed leaves, release $O_2$ by-product, store resins and gums in old dead xylem, and excrete into surrounding soil.
