Section 10.1 • Three Geometric Possibilities

Circles & Lines: Non-Intersecting, Secant, or Tangent

When a straight line PQ and a circle exist in the same plane, there are exactly three mutually exclusive possibilities for their common points!

⚙️
Thomas Fineke (1583)
From Latin tangere (to touch)

1. Non-Intersecting Line

0 Common Points: The line passes outside the circle without touching or cutting it.

2. Secant of Circle

2 Common Points (A & B): The line intersects the circle twice, containing a chord AB.

3. Tangent to Circle

Exactly 1 Common Point (A): The line touches the circle at one unique point of contact!

🪣
Well Pulley Rope (Fig. 10.2) The taut rope pulling water on both sides of a pulley forms two tangent rays touching the pulley wheel.
🚲
Bicycle Rolling Wheel (Fig. 10.4) The flat road is a tangent to the rolling wheel circle at the instant point of contact, with the spoke perpendicular to the ground!
Section 10.2 • Limiting Process

The Tangent: A Secant Whose Endpoints Coincide

A tangent is not an isolated object—it is the limiting position of a secant when the two endpoints of its corresponding chord move closer and closer until they merge into one single point!

Activity 1: Rotating Wire About P

Fig. 10.3(i)

Fix a point P on a circular wire and rotate a line AB around it. The second intersection point Q moves along the circumference until Q coincides with P!

Key Discovery:

There is one and only one tangent at any given point on a circle!

Activity 2: Parallel Secants

Fig. 10.3(ii)

Draw lines parallel to a secant PQ on both sides. The chord length cut by the lines gradually shrinks to zero at the opposite outer boundaries P'Q' and P''Q''.

Key Discovery:

A circle can have at most two parallel tangents for any given secant or diameter direction!

"The tangent to a circle is a special case of the secant, when the two end points of its corresponding chord coincide."
Theorem 10.1 • The Perpendicularity Law

Theorem 10.1: Tangent is Perpendicular to the Radius

The tangent at any point of a circle is strictly perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact: OP ⊥ XY.

Proof Geometry (Fig. 10.5)

The Shortest Distance Principle:
  1. Let XY be the tangent touching the circle at point P.
  2. Take any point Q on XY other than P.
  3. Q must lie outside the circle! (If Q were inside, XY would be a secant, not a tangent).
  4. Therefore, OQ > OP (radius).
  5. Since this holds for every point on XY except P, OP is the shortest distance from O to line XY!
  6. In Euclidean geometry, the shortest segment from a point to a line is the perpendicular.
OP ⊥ XY
💡 Definition of Normal: The straight line containing the radius through the point of contact is called the normal to the circle at that point.
Section 10.3 • Tangent Multiplicity

Number of Tangents from a Point (Inside, On, or Outside)

The position of point P relative to the circle strictly determines how many tangents can be drawn through it.

Case 1: Point P Inside Circle

0 Tangents

Any line through a point inside the circle intersects it at two points, forming a secant. No tangent is possible!

Case 2: Point P on Circle

Exactly 1 Tangent

There is one and only one unique tangent touching the circle at point P, perpendicular to radius OP.

Case 3: Point P Outside Circle

Exactly 2 Tangents

From an external point P, exactly two tangents PT₁ and PT₂ can be drawn!

Theorem 10.2 • The Grand Tangent Theorem

Theorem 10.2: Tangents from an External Point are Equal

"The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal." Proved via RHS Congruence and verified with Pythagoras!

Fig. 10.7 Setup (RHS Congruence)

Proof via RHS Congruence:
In right Δ OQP and right Δ ORP:
• OQ = OR (Radii of same circle)
• OP = OP (Common Hypotenuse)
• ∠OQP = ∠ORP = 90° (Theorem 10.1)
∴ Δ OQP ≅ Δ ORP (RHS Criterion)
➔ PQ = PR (CPCT)
Two Vital CPCT Properties:
  • 1. ∠OPQ = ∠OPR: Line OP is the angle bisector of ∠QPR (centre lies on the bisector of the angle between tangents).
  • 2. ∠POQ = ∠POR: Line OP bisects the central angle subtended by the chord of contact QR.
Examples 1 & 2 • Classical Geometric Deductions

Concentric Circles & The Angle Relation ∠PTQ = 2∠OPQ

Combining circle properties with tangent theorems to prove chord bisection and subtended angle relationships.

Textbook Example 1

Chord of Larger Circle Touching Smaller Circle is Bisected

Two concentric circles C₁ and C₂ with centre O. Chord AB of C₁ touches C₂ at point P.

• AB is a tangent to C₂ at P ➔ OP ⊥ AB (Thm 10.1)
• AB is a chord of larger circle C₁
• Perpendicular from centre to chord bisects the chord
∴ AP = BP (Bisected at P!)
Board application: Radii 5 cm and 3 cm ➔ AP = √(5² - 3²) = 4 cm ➔ Chord AB = 8 cm!
Textbook Example 2

Prove that ∠PTQ = 2 ∠OPQ (Fig. 10.9)

Let ∠PTQ = θ
Since TP = TQ (Thm 10.2), Δ TPQ is isosceles.
∠TPQ = ∠TQP = ½(180° - θ) = 90° - θ/2
Since OP ⊥ PT, ∠OPT = 90°
∠OPQ = ∠OPT - ∠TPQ = 90° - (90° - θ/2) = θ/2
∴ ∠PTQ = 2 ∠OPQ ✔️
Crucial board exam result: The angle between two tangents is double the angle between chord and radius!
Example 3 • Similarity & Tangent Length

Finding Tangent Length TP (Chord 8cm, Radius 5cm)

"PQ is a chord of length 8 cm of a circle of radius 5 cm. Tangents at P and Q intersect at T. Find length TP." (Fig. 10.10)

Fig. 10.10 Geometry

Method 1: By AA Similarity (Fastest)
OT ⊥ PQ and bisects PQ ➔ PR = RQ = 4 cm
In right Δ PRO: OR = √(5² - 4²) = 3 cm
∠TPR + ∠RPO = 90° and ∠PTR + ∠TPR = 90°
∴ ∠RPO = ∠PTR ➔ Δ TRP ~ Δ PRO (AA)
TP / PO = RP / RO
TP / 5 = 4 / 3 ➔ TP = 20 / 3 cm
Length TP = 20/3 cm ≈ 6.67 cm
Exercise 10.2 Q8 & Q11 • Circumscribed Figures

Quadrilateral Circumscribing a Circle: AB + CD = AD + BC

When a polygon circumscribes a circle, every vertex acts as an external point with two equal tangent segments. Adding all four vertices yields the grand perimeter property!

Fig. 10.12 Tangent Segments

Equal Tangent Segments by Theorem 10.2:
AP = AS (1)
BP = BQ (2)
CR = CQ (3)
DR = DS (4)
Adding equations (1) + (2) + (3) + (4):
(AP + BP) + (CR + DR) = (AS + DS) + (BQ + CQ)
AB + CD = AD + BC ✔️
Q11 Corollary: Parallelogram Circumscribing Circle

In a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal (AB = CD and AD = BC).
Substituting into AB + CD = AD + BC gives 2AB = 2AD ➔ AB = AD.
A parallelogram with adjacent sides equal is a RHOMBUS!

Exercise 10.2 Q12 • The Ultimate 5-Mark Question

Triangle Circumscribing a Circle (r = 4 cm, BD = 8, DC = 6)

Finding sides AB and AC of triangle ABC circumscribing a circle of radius 4 cm when BC is split into 8 cm and 6 cm by the point of contact D!

Fig. 10.14 Setup

Let tangent segments from A be AF = AE = x
Sides: a = 14, b = 6 + x, c = 8 + x
Semi-perimeter: s = (14 + 6+x + 8+x)/2 = 14 + x
Area by Decomposition = Area(OAB) + Area(OBC) + Area(OCA)
Area = ½·r·(a + b + c) = r·s = 4(14 + x)
Area by Heron's Formula:
√[(14+x)(x)(8)(6)] = 4(14 + x)
Square both sides: 48x(14 + x) = 16(14 + x)²
3x = 14 + x ➔ 2x = 14 ➔ x = 7 cm
AB = 8 + 7 = 15 cm, AC = 6 + 7 = 13 cm
AB = 15 cm & AC = 13 cm
